Symbol Corporate-Action Adjustment

A symbol adjustment keeps venue prices, stored quotes, unrealized profit and loss (UPNL), and funding on one unit basis when a split, reverse split, or rebase changes the number of units.

The unit-basis problem

A unit-changing venue event changes quantity and price together. A 4:1 split turns one old unit into four new units, while the venue price becomes about one quarter of its old value. The economic position has not changed.

Core does not automatically rewrite an open quote when the venue changes. If a quote still stores one unit opened at 400, comparing it with the venue's new-unit price of 100 produces a false loss. The same mismatch would affect notional, funding per unit, liquidation prices, and any signature that carries a price or UPNL.

Core maintains one invariant:

Every quantity and every price or per-unit rate used with it must use the same unit basis.

Two routes maintain that invariant. On the adjusted-price route, Core leaves quote storage alone and only stores and exposes the factor. Muon and PartyB integrations must convert external prices and funding inputs back to the stored quote basis. On the physical route, Core freezes execution and converts eligible quote storage to the venue's new basis. The mechanism covers splits, reverse splits, and quantity rebases. It does not model cash dividends, spin-offs, mergers, ticker migrations, or normal price moves.

Basis and factor vocabulary

The venue feed reports a price in the units currently traded by the venue. This page calls that the raw venue basis. A quote's fields use the units currently held in Core storage. This is the stored quote basis. They are the same before an adjustment and after a complete physical restatement. They differ while an adjustment factor is active.

Term Meaning
factor The latest scheduled new-units-per-old-unit multiplier, scaled by 1e18. A 4:1 split is 4e18; a 1:10 reverse split is 0.1e18.
Active factor getCumulativeFactor. It is the product of confirmed factors that external prices must apply because quote storage has not absorbed them. Raw zero storage means unset and this view returns 1e18.
Prospective factor getProspectiveCumulativeFactor. While a step is SCHEDULED, it is the active factor compounded with the scheduled factor. Otherwise it equals the active factor.
restatementFactor The factor selected when a physical restatement window opens. Direct restatement selects the prospective factor. A later restatement selects the active factor.
Freeze A computed condition, not a lifecycle state. The symbol is frozen while restating is true, or while state is SCHEDULED and block.timestamp >= effectiveTimestamp.
Physical restatement A maintenance window in which eligible stored quotes are rewritten to the raw venue basis, current funding rates are paused and rebased, and basisVersion advances on finalization.

Factors compound with floor rounding:

prospectiveFactor = floor(activeFactor * scheduledFactor / 1e18)

For example, a confirmed 2e18 factor followed by a scheduled 3e18 step gives a prospective factor of 6e18. Direct restatement uses 6e18 while the active factor remains 2e18. Confirmation would instead make 6e18 active. Scheduling does not calculate the prospective product. It can therefore accept a step whose product later floors to zero or whose quotient exceeds uint256. The prospective getter reports zero, but it reverts on overflow. Confirmation, direct start, and preview reject zero and also propagate an overflow revert. Tests reach one wei after nine successive 0.01x confirmations, schedule the tenth step, and reject its confirmation.

Muon is the off-chain oracle and signature service. PartyA is the trader, and PartyB is the solver or hedger. An address with SYMBOL_MANAGER_ROLE makes the lifecycle calls. This page uses Operations for the people and systems coordinating that role, Muon, and the off-chain inventory checks.

One split, two route outcomes

Use one 4:1 split to compare the two routes. Before the event, an open long has one unit opened at 400. Its stored current funding rate is 8 per old unit. At the event, the venue changes to four units priced at 100 each. 

Before the event                     Raw venue basis after the event
quantity       = 1                   quantity equivalent = 4
openedPrice    = 400                 raw venue price      = 100
notional       = 1 * 400 = 400       notional             = 4 * 100 = 400
funding amount = 1 * 8 = 8

If Core used the raw price 100 with the old stored quantity and opened price, the long's price UPNL would be 1 * (100 - 400) = -300. The correct UPNL at the unchanged economic price is zero.

On the adjusted-price route, Muon multiplies the raw venue price by the active factor. Core sees an old-unit mark of 100 * 4 = 400, matching the unchanged quote. Quantity, notional, UPNL, and funding all remain on the old basis.

On the physical route, Core rewrites the quote to four units opened at 100. The current funding rate is restored as 8 / 4 = 2 per new unit. The products remain 4 * 100 = 400 notional and 4 * 2 = 8 funding. After finalization, Muon can use the raw 100 price without conversion.

What happens after scheduling

A manager schedules a step with scheduleAdjustment(symbolId, factor, effectiveTimestamp). The symbol stays live before a future effective time. It freezes automatically at equality, without a keeper transaction. Core accepts a past effective time, so an emergency schedule can freeze in the scheduling transaction. Core records that transaction time separately in adjustmentScheduledAt.

The factor must be between 1e16 and 100e18, inclusive, and cannot equal 1e18. Only one SCHEDULED step may be in flight, and no schedule may be added during a restatement window. A new step can be scheduled after PRICE_ADJUSTED, APPLIED, or CANCELLED. Existing active factors survive the new schedule.

Scheduling does not choose a route. Once the venue outcome is known, Operations either cancels a called-off event or handles an event that happened through one of two routes.

If the venue event is called off

Call cancelAdjustment. This is an exit from the lifecycle, not a third adjustment route, because there is no unit change to handle.

Cancel and abort do different jobs

Action When it is available Result
cancelAdjustment The state is SCHEDULED and no restatement window is open. Marks the latest schedule CANCELLED. The symbol stays live or becomes live again. Any older active factor remains in use.
abortRestatement A restatement window is open, but no quote has been rewritten or removed from PartyA's pending inventory. Closes the window, restores paused funding rates, and returns to the state that opened the window.

After a direct-route abort, the state is still effective SCHEDULED, so the symbol remains frozen. Operations must restart restatement, switch to the adjusted-price route with confirmPriceAdjusted, or cancel if the event was called off. An abort from a later window normally returns to live PRICE_ADJUSTED. Core rejects abort once a quote rewrite or PartyA pending removal sets restatementMutated. Other quote-state changes do not always set this flag, and abort neither detects nor reverses them. Operations must reconcile the book before aborting. An aborted window's epoch is not reused.

Adjusted-price route without quote rewrites

This route resumes trading without rewriting quotes. Muon first changes the external price basis while quote storage stays unchanged. At or after the effective time and before confirmation, the feed factor is the prospective factor. After confirmation, it is the active factor: 

adjusted old-unit price = floor(raw venue price * feed factor / 1e18)
venue quantity          = floor(stored quantity * feed factor / 1e18)
venue closed amount     = floor(stored closed amount * feed factor / 1e18)
venue open amount       = venue quantity - venue closed amount

Core scales total and closed amounts separately. Their difference can be one amount wei away from floor(stored open amount * feed factor / 1e18).

  1. At the effective time, confirm that isSymbolFrozen(symbolId) is true.
  2. Muon reads getProspectiveCumulativeFactor and starts producing old-unit adjusted prices.
  3. Operations verifies the feed basis independently.
  4. Operations calls confirmPriceAdjusted.

Confirmation changes state to PRICE_ADJUSTED, writes the prospective value to cumulativeFactor, and removes the scheduled freeze. Stored quotes do not change. Muon and PartyBs must continue using old-unit adjusted prices while that factor is active. This includes the marketPrices input used for funding updates. Core does not apply the factor to that input or validate its basis.

The active factor may remain in use. If Operations later chooses physical restatement, it opens a new frozen window with startRestatement. The window selects the active cumulative factor, not merely the latest scheduled step.

Physical route with quote restatement

Physical restatement can start directly from an effective SCHEDULED step or later while a non-1x active factor exists. The direct route selects the prospective factor but does not activate it in cumulativeFactor. The later route selects the active factor. In both cases, Core increments restatementEpoch, sets restating, saves restatementFactor, and pauses eligible funding rates.

The implementation does not restrict a later start specifically to PRICE_ADJUSTED. Outside SCHEDULED, startRestatement succeeds whenever the normalized active factor is nonzero and not 1e18. One practical case is CANCELLED after a newer scheduled step was cancelled on top of an older active factor.

  1. Establish and record the old-basis Muon issuance cutoff, then open the window with startRestatement.
  2. Re-scan the frozen book before any mutation. Abort if any quote cannot be handled.
  3. Force-expire eligible pending quotes and rewrite eligible open quotes in batches.
  4. Reconcile every quote and funding pair. Core does not do this inventory check.
  5. Wait strictly past both the on-chain time gate and the proven old-basis signature-expiry deadline, then finalize.

Finalization rebases checkpointed funding, writes state APPLIED only when the prior state was SCHEDULED or PRICE_ADJUSTED, sets cumulativeFactor to an explicit 1e18, closes the window, clears restatementFactor and restatementStartedAt, and increments basisVersion. It retains the latest per-step factor and effectiveTimestamp.

Finalization does not prove full-book coverage. It does not require a quote count, a pending-quote count, or even one mutation. A manager can finalize after the time gate with eligible old-basis quotes still present, and basisVersion will still increment. Complete coverage is an operational obligation.

What physical restatement changes

Eligibility, authorization, and batch behavior

applyAdjustment(symbolId, quoteIds) requires an open window. A manager may process any quote. A PartyB may process only its own quote, and authorization is checked separately for every ID. Each quote must match the symbol, have an epoch older than the current window, and have status OPENED, CLOSE_PENDING, or CANCEL_CLOSE_PENDING. PartyA must not be liquidating, and PartyB must not be liquidating against that PartyA.

The call is atomic. Any invalid ID, failed funding charge, arithmetic failure, aggregate overflow, or downstream revert rolls back every earlier quote in the batch. A duplicate epoch reverts with Already restated. Repeating a completed ID reverts, so retries must omit completed IDs. An empty array is accepted and changes nothing.

Conversion arithmetic and fields

previewQuoteAdjustment uses the same arithmetic and underflow checks as the write path. It checks the symbol, but it does not check quote status, liquidation state, authorization, or whether funding settlement will succeed.

Factors and unit-bearing quote values use 18 decimals. Math.mulDiv floors each result.

new amount = floor(old amount * factor / 1e18)
new price  = 0                                           when old price is 0
new price  = floor(old amount * old price / new amount) otherwise
Amount basis Fields rewritten
Old and new total quantity quantity, openedPrice, initialOpenedPrice, requestedOpenPrice, and marketPrice
Old and new closedAmount closedAmount and avgClosedPrice
Old and new quantityToClose quantityToClose and requestedClosePrice

The conversion assigns exactly those nine fields. It does not assign IDs, parties, symbol, position or order type, whitelists, statuses, deadlines, parent links, locked values, fee fields, affiliate, funding limit, close fee, or data. Funding settlement runs before conversion on every successful applyAdjustment. It sets lastFundingPaymentTimestamp to the current block time and may change accumulatedPaidFunding and party balances. These are settlement side effects, not part of the nine-field unit conversion.

The arithmetic rejects a new total quantity of zero, a nonzero closed amount that becomes zero, a new quantity less than or equal to the new closed amount, and a nonzero requested close amount that becomes zero. For a valid price conversion, the exact dust bound is: 

0 <= oldAmount * oldPrice - newAmount * newPrice < newAmount

This means the total raw notional dust is less than the new amount, equivalent to less than one price wei per new unit on average. It does not mean total notional dust is less than one wei.

Funding settlement and aggregate updates

For each quote, the facet currently executes this order:

  1. Prepare the quote's PartyB funding pair if the window did not checkpoint it.
  2. Charge the quote's accumulated funding on the old basis.
  3. Remove old open amount and opened-price notional from PartyB global, PartyB per-PartyA, and PartyA per-PartyB position aggregates. The helper also subtracts the old weighted-funding contribution.
  4. Subtract the old weighted-funding contribution a second time in the facet, as described below.
  5. Rewrite the nine unit-bearing quote fields.
  6. Add the new position aggregates and one new weighted-funding contribution.
  7. Stamp quoteRestatedEpoch, set the mutation flag, and emit QuoteAdjusted.
The v0.8.6 write path subtracts weighted funding twice. LibQuote.subFromPartiesAggregatedPositions already calls LibAggregateFunding.subFromPartiesAggregateFunding. _restateQuote then calls the same subtraction explicitly before adding the new contribution once. For an old contribution C and new contribution C', the stored result changes by -2C + C', not -C + C'. Do not claim weighted aggregate funding preservation from the current source or existing position-aggregate tests. A physical-restatement procedure must account for this implementation defect before it is used on funded quotes.

Pending inventory

cancelPendingQuotes is manager-only. It does not require an open restatement window, but every quote's symbol must be frozen. It accepts PENDING, LOCKED, and CANCEL_PENDING. Core force-expires each quote without the normal deadline check, while retaining both liquidation guards. It refunds the open trading fee, releases pending locks and PartyB connections where applicable, removes pending indexes, sets the quote to EXPIRED, and calls cancellation hooks. A hook revert rolls back the batch. An empty quote array is accepted and changes nothing.

Removing a quote from PartyA's pending index records a mutation if a restatement window is open. This applies to cancelPendingQuotes and to normal user-driven pending removal. A cancellation while the symbol is merely frozen, before a window opens, does not mark a future window. Operations must monitor normal cancellation events during a window because the first such removal also makes abort unavailable. Close-request creation, cancellation, and expiry can also change the quote inventory during the window, but those paths do not set the restatement mutation flag.

Mixed-book reads

Raw storage is mixed while batches run. getQuote returns that storage unchanged. getQuoteInVenueUnits and getQuotesInVenueUnits return converted memory copies that can support display, valuation, reconciliation, and hedging when the selected factor represents the intended venue basis. They must not be sent back into Core execution.

Quote condition factorApplied in venue-unit view
Restated in the current open epoch 1e18
Not yet restated in the current open epoch restatementFactor
No open window Active cumulative factor

During the gap after a scheduled adjustment becomes effective but before confirmation or startRestatement, no window is open and these views still apply only the active factor. They ignore the scheduled prospective factor. Their result is therefore not normalized to the post-event venue basis during this gap. To normalize it, convert raw getQuote storage once with getProspectiveCumulativeFactor. Do not apply the full prospective factor to a venue-unit view that already includes an older active factor. Otherwise, suppress affected output until Operations confirms a route transition.

The views do not filter by quote status. When the selected factor is not 1e18, they use the same conversion and underflow checks as preview. A fully closed quote reverts because its converted quantity is not greater than its converted closed amount; amount-rounding underflows can also revert. The array getter is atomic, so one non-convertible ID reverts the entire batch. Use bounded batches with a single-ID fallback, and use raw getQuote for closed history or any record that cannot be converted.

The venue-unit view also returns the current epoch, freeze status, and storedInVenueUnits. That last flag means only that this view applied 1e18. It is not independent proof of a quote's provenance or coverage.

Funding across the window

When a window opens, Core scans the entire current MAStorage.partyBList. For each PartyB, it skips a symbol pair if epochDuration is zero or both current rates are zero. Otherwise it rolls accumulated rates to one cutoff, saves the current long and short rates with the window epoch, adds the PartyB to the per-symbol restoration list, and writes both current rates to zero. A saved pair can have one zero side and one nonzero side.

A deregistered PartyB is not in the global list. If one of its quotes is rewritten, applyAdjustment checkpoints that PartyB lazily before settling the quote. This fallback depends on processing at least one of its quotes. If Operations omits all of that PartyB's quotes, an active rate can remain uncheckpointed and unre-based when the manager finalizes.

Abort restores checkpointed rates without rolling the zero-rate interval. In cumulative accounting, the restored rates therefore count as if they had remained active throughout the aborted window. Finalization first rolls the zero-rate interval, then rebases each saved rate: 

newRate = sign(oldRate) * floor(abs(oldRate) * 1e18 / restatementFactor)

Flooring can reduce the restored magnitude. Core rejects a magnitude that does not fit in int256. It then deletes each checkpoint and the per-symbol PartyB restoration list.

Funding preparation and restoration are not batched. startRestatement is O(all currently registered PartyBs), including skipped pairs. finalizeRestatement is O(checkpointed PartyBs). Both loops do storage work and emit events. Rehearse both calls against a current state snapshot. If either cannot fit in the block gas limit, do not open a restatement window. Keep the active-factor route in service instead.

Enforced and operational boundaries

What the freeze enforces

The freeze is not a global symbol pause. Internal requireNotFrozen checks block paths that resolve the affected symbol. In v0.8.6 those checks cover:

  • quote creation, PartyB open and close execution, batch wrappers, and solver-fee wrappers;
  • legacy and unified settlement;
  • force actions, three-step force-close finalization, emergency PartyB actions, clearing-house closes, and ADL-style closes;
  • PartyA liquidation price snapshot setup in new and legacy paths, and PartyB position liquidation;
  • funding charge, rate setting, epoch-duration changes, and accumulated-funding paths.

The freeze leaves several quote mutations callable. PartyA can call requestToClosePosition, which changes an open quote to CLOSE_PENDING and writes the requested close price, close quantity, order type, deadline, and modification timestamp. PartyA can cancel a close request, and anyone can pass an expired close request to expireQuote; neither path checks the symbol freeze. The frozen manifest is therefore not static. Operations must monitor and reconcile close-request creation, cancellation, and expiry throughout batching.

Other non-price cleanup can remain callable. A function that neither receives a symbol nor resolves one from a quote cannot apply the freeze check. The freeze does not cancel an existing liquidation state. Already-liquidating accounts make affected quotes ineligible for applyAdjustment. The tests cover many freeze gates, but they do not prove a complete liquidation-state recovery matrix for physical restatement.

Signature expiry and account-level payloads

Muon payloads do not carry basisVersion. Core uses a time gate, rather than a payload version, to separate old and new bases. Finalization requires: 

block.timestamp > restatementStartedAt + LibMuon.maxUpnlValidTime()

Signature verification accepts the exact expiry boundary and rejects only after it. Finalization uses the strict greater-than check, so equality still fails. A zero per-function validity override falls back to the global validity. maxUpnlValidTime() takes the maximum of the global setting and every enum override, using the live configuration when finalization runs.

For direct restatement, restatementStartedAt is the later of effectiveTimestamp and adjustmentScheduledAt. This prevents a past-effective emergency schedule from backdating the wait before its scheduling transaction, but it does not prove that off-chain issuance stopped then. For a later restatement opened while the symbol is live, restatementStartedAt is the block time of startRestatement.

Account-level UPNL payloads do not list every symbol in the account. Core cannot infer that such a payload includes exposure to the frozen symbol, so Muon must stop issuing affected account-level signatures off-chain. The cutoff must cover every affected account-, pair-, and quote-level payload. Record the latest old-basis signature timestamp, or prove an upper bound from the issuance cutoff and allowed signer clock skew. Core checks each signature's timestamp and does not reject a future timestamp.

The on-chain finalization deadline is sufficient only when the proven latest old-basis timestamp is no later than restatementStartedAt. Otherwise Operations must wait until the block time is strictly greater than the latest old-basis timestamp bound plus the maximum applicable validity, even if finalizeRestatement would already succeed. A delayed cutoff is not detected by successful finalization.

Governance and Operations must also prevent a later validity increase from reviving an old-basis signature. Before applying a new global or per-function validity, require the current block time to be strictly greater than the latest timestamp bound for the affected old-basis signatures plus the proposed applicable validity. The recorded freeze start is a safe substitute only when the cutoff proof shows that no old-basis timestamp can be later than that start.

basisVersion and deferred force close

Finalization increments basisVersion, even if no quote was processed. The three-step force-close flow records this value at initialization. Its refresh step validates a fresh signature but deliberately does not replace the stored close price or basis version. Finalization of that force close requires the symbol to be unfrozen and the stored version to match, so an old-basis workflow must be initialized again. This protection is specific to that three-step flow. It is not a generic field in Muon signatures or every deferred workflow.

Revert-worthy boundaries

  • Schedule rejects an unknown symbol, a factor outside [1e16, 100e18], factor 1e18, an open window, or another SCHEDULED step.
  • Any call that computes a prospective factor can revert if the quotient exceeds uint256. Confirm and direct start also reject a call before effectiveTimestamp and reject a prospective factor of zero.
  • Start rejects an existing window and normalized factor 1e18.
  • Abort rejects no window or any window whose mutation flag is true.
  • Apply rejects authorization, symbol, epoch, status, and liquidation failures, plus funding, arithmetic, or aggregate failures reached downstream.
  • Pending cancellation rejects an unfrozen symbol, an ineligible status, liquidation guards, or a reverting hook.
  • Finalize rejects no window and the exact or earlier signature-expiry boundary. Funding checkpoint mismatch, accumulated-rate work, or signed-rate overflow can also revert. It does not reject incomplete inventory.

Operations runbook

Prepare and schedule

  1. Verify the venue notice, symbol ID, factor direction, effective time, and allowed factor range.
  2. Read the active factor and compute the intended prospective product before scheduling. Require 0 < floor(activeFactor * intendedFactor / 1e18) <= type(uint256).max. After scheduling, confirm that the prospective getter reports the same value without reverting. Do not infer the new basis from only the latest step.
  3. Build one open and pending quote manifest across all PartyB account models. Snapshot liquidation and funding state.
  4. Run previewQuoteAdjustment for every eligible open quote. Treat this as an arithmetic preview, not an eligibility or funding-settlement proof.
  5. Measure the current registered PartyB list and checkpoint candidates. Fork-rehearse both unbatched funding loops.
  6. Resolve the duplicate weighted-funding subtraction risk before approving any physical route on funded inventory.
  7. Verify the deployed ABI uses startRestatement(uint256). The old three-argument selector is not exposed.
  8. Call scheduleAdjustment. Record the transaction block time, adjustment index, factor, effective time, prospective factor, and current maximum Muon validity.

Reach the effective boundary

  1. Refresh the manifest before the effective time. Trading is still live for a future schedule.
  2. For a future schedule, stop every affected old-basis account-, pair-, and quote-level Muon payload before the effective time. For a past-effective emergency, stop issuance before submitting the schedule transaction. Record the latest observed signed timestamp and compute an upper bound that includes the issuance cutoff plus allowed signer clock skew. Prove that no later old-basis timestamp can be issued.
  3. At equality or later, verify isSymbolFrozen and the raw venue feed basis.
  4. If the event did not occur, call cancelAdjustment. Verify the symbol unfreezes and the older active factor remains unchanged.

Use the adjusted-price route

  1. Have Muon compute old-unit prices from getProspectiveCumulativeFactor.
  2. Compare independent calculations with the feed and running notional and UPNL checks.
  3. Call confirmPriceAdjusted. Verify PRICE_ADJUSTED, the expected active factor, and unfreeze.
  4. Resume Muon signatures and PartyB funding inputs in the adjusted old-unit basis.

Use either physical-restatement route

  1. Keep affected signatures stopped. Call startRestatement(symbolId) and record epoch, factor, start time, gas, checkpoint events, and zeroed current rates.
  2. Re-scan the now-frozen book before the first mutation. Recheck statuses, every liquidation mode, previews, funding settlement, hooks, and all deregistered PartyBs with open quotes.
  3. If any item cannot be processed, abort before a quote rewrite or pending removal. Direct abort stays frozen in SCHEDULED. Active-factor abort preserves the prior state, normally live PRICE_ADJUSTED, but it can also return live in CANCELLED.
  4. Force-expire all eligible pending inventory. Monitor normal pending cancellation and close-request creation, cancellation, and expiry throughout the window. Reconcile each resulting status and price-bearing close field before processing the quote.
  5. Apply open quotes in bounded batches. Remove successful IDs before retrying. Track QuoteAdjusted and verify each quote's epoch.
  6. Use venue-unit views for convertible records during the open window, but compare raw storage and epoch data to prove physical coverage. Keep batches bounded, retry failed batches one ID at a time, and use raw getQuote for closed or non-convertible history.
  7. Re-scan the complete manifest. Confirm no eligible old-epoch quote or unresolved pending quote remains. Reconcile position aggregates, funding checkpoints, and weighted aggregate funding explicitly.
  8. Lock Muon validity configuration. Prove that the old-basis timestamp upper bound is no later than restatementStartedAt. If it is later, extend the operational deadline to that upper bound plus the maximum applicable validity. Wait strictly past both that deadline and the on-chain restatementStartedAt + maxUpnlValidTime() deadline.
  9. Call finalizeRestatement. Do not treat success as signature-expiry or coverage proof.
  10. Verify the expected lifecycle state, explicit 1e18 cumulative factor, closed window, incremented basisVersion, deleted checkpoints, rebased rates, and unfreeze.
  11. Resume Muon with raw venue prices. Reinitialize stale three-step force closes and retain the old-basis timestamp cutoff proof. Before any later validity increase, wait strictly past that timestamp bound plus the proposed applicable validity.

The read functions are unrestricted. getSymbolAdjustment returns raw record values. getCumulativeFactor normalizes unset zero. getProspectiveCumulativeFactor includes a scheduled step only in SCHEDULED. isSymbolFrozen evaluates the live predicate. getRestatementState returns the window flag and epoch. getQuoteRestatedEpoch returns a quote's last physical epoch. previewQuoteAdjustment selects the open-window, prospective, or active factor and returns the exact nine converted fields.

Storage retains only the latest schedule, so indexers need these events for complete adjustment history. Funding pause, abort restore, and finalization rebase use the existing AccumulatedFundingStateUpdated event for each affected symbol and PartyB pair. Every successful applyAdjustment also emits QuoteFundingSettled before QuoteAdjusted, including when the settled funding amount is zero. Indexers need both events to attribute the settlement side effects and the later unit conversion.
