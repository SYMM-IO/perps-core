Symbol Corporate-Action Adjustment

A symbol adjustment keeps venue prices, stored quotes, unrealized profit and loss (UPNL), and funding on one unit basis when a split, reverse split, or rebase changes the number of units.

The unit-basis problem

A unit-changing venue event changes quantity and price together. A 4:1 split turns one old unit into four new units, while the venue price becomes about one quarter of its old value. The economic position has not changed.

Core does not automatically rewrite an open quote when the venue changes. If a quote still stores one unit opened at 400, comparing it with the venue's new-unit price of 100 produces a false loss. The same mismatch would affect notional, funding per unit, liquidation prices, and any signature that carries a price or UPNL.

Core maintains one invariant:

Every quantity and every price or per-unit rate used with it must use the same unit basis.

Two routes maintain that invariant. On the adjusted-price route, Core leaves quote storage alone and only stores and exposes the factor. Muon and PartyB integrations must convert external prices and funding inputs back to the stored quote basis. On the physical route, Core freezes execution and converts eligible quote storage to the venue's new basis. The mechanism covers splits, reverse splits, and quantity rebases. It does not model cash dividends, spin-offs, mergers, ticker migrations, or normal price moves.

Basis and factor vocabulary

The venue feed reports a price in the units currently traded by the venue. This page calls that the raw venue basis. A quote's fields use the units currently held in Core storage. This is the stored quote basis. They are the same before an adjustment and after a complete physical restatement. They differ while an adjustment factor is active.

Term Meaning
factor The latest scheduled new-units-per-old-unit multiplier, scaled by 1e18. A 4:1 split is 4e18; a 1:10 reverse split is 0.1e18.
Active factor getCumulativeFactor. It is the product of confirmed factors that external prices must apply because quote storage has not absorbed them. Raw zero storage means unset and this view returns 1e18.
Prospective factor getProspectiveCumulativeFactor. While a step is SCHEDULED, it is the active factor compounded with the scheduled factor. Otherwise it equals the active factor.
restatementFactor The factor selected when a physical restatement window opens. Direct restatement selects the prospective factor. A later restatement selects the active factor.
Freeze A computed condition, not a lifecycle state. The symbol is frozen while restating is true, or while state is SCHEDULED and block.timestamp >= effectiveTimestamp.
Physical restatement A maintenance window in which eligible stored quotes are rewritten to the raw venue basis, current funding rates are paused and rebased, and basisVersion advances on finalization.

Factors compound with floor rounding:

prospectiveFactor = floor(activeFactor * scheduledFactor / 1e18)

For example, a confirmed 2e18 factor followed by a scheduled 3e18 step gives a prospective factor of 6e18. Direct restatement uses 6e18 while the active factor remains 2e18. Confirmation would instead make 6e18 active. Scheduling does not calculate the prospective product. It can therefore accept a step whose product later floors to zero or whose quotient exceeds uint256. The prospective getter reports zero, but it reverts on overflow. Confirmation, direct start, and preview reject zero and also propagate an overflow revert. Tests reach one wei after nine successive 0.01x confirmations, schedule the tenth step, and reject its confirmation.

Muon is the off-chain oracle and signature service. PartyA is the trader, and PartyB is the solver or hedger. An address with SYMBOL_MANAGER_ROLE makes the lifecycle calls. This page uses Operations for the people and systems coordinating that role, Muon, and the off-chain inventory checks.

One split, two route outcomes

Use one 4:1 split to compare the two routes. Before the event, an open long has one unit opened at 400. Its stored current funding rate is 8 per old unit. At the event, the venue changes to four units priced at 100 each. 

Before the event                     Raw venue basis after the event
quantity       = 1                   quantity equivalent = 4
openedPrice    = 400                 raw venue price      = 100
notional       = 1 * 400 = 400       notional             = 4 * 100 = 400
funding amount = 1 * 8 = 8

If Core used the raw price 100 with the old stored quantity and opened price, the long's price UPNL would be 1 * (100 - 400) = -300. The correct UPNL at the unchanged economic price is zero.

On the adjusted-price route, Muon multiplies the raw venue price by the active factor. Core sees an old-unit mark of 100 * 4 = 400, matching the unchanged quote. Quantity, notional, UPNL, and funding all remain on the old basis.

On the physical route, Core rewrites the quote to four units opened at 100. The current funding rate is restored as 8 / 4 = 2 per new unit. The products remain 4 * 100 = 400 notional and 4 * 2 = 8 funding. After finalization, Muon can use the raw 100 price without conversion.

What happens after scheduling

A manager schedules a step with scheduleAdjustment(symbolId, factor, effectiveTimestamp). The symbol stays live before a future effective time. It freezes automatically at equality, without a keeper transaction. Core accepts a past effective time, so an emergency schedule can freeze in the scheduling transaction. Core records that transaction time separately in adjustmentScheduledAt.

The factor must be between 1e16 and 100e18, inclusive, and cannot equal 1e18. Only one SCHEDULED step may be in flight, and no schedule may be added during a restatement window. A new step can be scheduled after PRICE_ADJUSTED, APPLIED, or CANCELLED. Existing active factors survive the new schedule.

Scheduling does not choose a route. Once the venue outcome is known, Operations either cancels a called-off event or handles an event that happened through one of two routes.

If the venue event is called off

Call cancelAdjustment. This is an exit from the lifecycle, not a third adjustment route, because there is no unit change to handle.

Cancel and abort do different jobs

Action When it is available Result
cancelAdjustment The state is SCHEDULED and no restatement window is open. Marks the latest schedule CANCELLED. The symbol stays live or becomes live again. Any older active factor remains in use.
abortRestatement A restatement window is open, but no quote has been rewritten or removed from PartyA's pending inventory. Starts funding restoration. If checkpoints exist, Operations supplies their PartyB addresses to processRestatementFunding; the last batch closes the window and emits RestatementAborted.

After a direct-route abort, the state is still effective SCHEDULED, so the symbol remains frozen. Operations must restart restatement, switch to the adjusted-price route with confirmPriceAdjusted, or cancel if the event was called off. An abort from a later window normally returns to live PRICE_ADJUSTED. Core rejects abort once a quote rewrite or PartyA pending removal sets restatementMutated. Other quote-state changes do not always set this flag, and abort neither detects nor reverses them. Operations must reconcile the book before aborting. An aborted window's epoch is not reused.

Adjusted-price route without quote rewrites

This route resumes trading without rewriting quotes. Muon first changes the external price basis while quote storage stays unchanged. At or after the effective time and before confirmation, the feed factor is the prospective factor. After confirmation, it is the active factor: 

adjusted old-unit price = floor(raw venue price * feed factor / 1e18)
venue quantity          = floor(stored quantity * feed factor / 1e18)
venue closed amount     = floor(stored closed amount * feed factor / 1e18)
venue open amount       = venue quantity - venue closed amount

The getQuoteInVenueUnits view, and its batch form, return normalized copies by scaling stored total and closed amounts separately. Their difference can be one amount wei away from floor(stored open amount * feed factor / 1e18). This view conversion does not modify quote storage. Normal Core execution still expects the stored old-unit values on this route.

  1. At the effective time, confirm that isSymbolFrozen(symbolId) is true.
  2. Muon reads getProspectiveCumulativeFactor and starts producing old-unit adjusted prices.
  3. Operations verifies the feed basis independently.
  4. Operations calls confirmPriceAdjusted.
Readiness is coordinated off-chain. Core has no on-chain readiness flow for Muon or PartyB. An Operations bot can perform those checks off-chain and call confirmPriceAdjusted after Muon is producing old-unit adjusted prices and PartyB systems are prepared to translate between venue units and Core's stored units. The call activates the factor and removes the scheduled freeze immediately.

Confirmation changes state to PRICE_ADJUSTED, writes the prospective value to cumulativeFactor, and removes the scheduled freeze. Stored quotes do not change. Muon and PartyBs must continue using old-unit adjusted prices while that factor is active. This includes the marketPrices input used for funding updates. Core does not apply the factor to that input or validate its basis.

The active factor may remain in use. If Operations later chooses physical restatement, it opens a new frozen window with startRestatement. The window selects the active cumulative factor, not merely the latest scheduled step.

Physical route with quote restatement

Physical restatement can start directly from an effective SCHEDULED step or later while a non-1x active factor exists. The direct route selects the prospective factor but does not activate it in cumulativeFactor. The later route selects the active factor. In both cases, startRestatement increments restatementEpoch, sets restating, saves restatementFactor, and records one funding cutoff. It does not scan the PartyB registry. It always opens FUNDING_PREPARATION. Operations supplies every involved PartyB in bounded batches so Core can snapshot open-position totals and, when accumulated funding is active, checkpoint funding rates. Operations then seals the submitted manifest before quote processing becomes available.

startRestatement can also be called from CANCELLED when an older non-1x factor is still active. For example, Operations may confirm one adjustment, schedule a newer adjustment, and then cancel only the newer step. Cancellation changes the state to CANCELLED but does not clear the earlier cumulativeFactor, so startRestatement can still open a physical-restatement window using that older factor.

Operations sequence for physical restatement

The paragraphs above explain when physical restatement can start and which factor it uses. Once Operations chooses this route, it follows the sequence below. Some steps are off-chain checks; the named functions are on-chain transactions.

  1. Find quotes that cannot survive the conversion. Operations runs previewQuoteAdjustment against every eligible open quote. When amount rounding makes a quote unrestatable, Operations marks it for PartyB cleanup once the symbol is frozen.
  2. Open the restatement window. Operations calls startRestatement. Core freezes the symbol, records the factor and funding cutoff for this window, and emits RestatementStarted. This transaction does not iterate over registered PartyBs.
  3. Prepare every involved PartyB. Operations builds the complete PartyB set off-chain from its open book and funding records. The bot simulates and submits processRestatementFunding(symbolId, partyBs) in gas-bounded address batches. Core snapshots LONG and SHORT totals for only those addresses and checkpoints funding when active. After reconciling the complete set, Operations calls completeRestatementFundingPreparation to seal it and move to QUOTE_PROCESSING.
  4. Close every marked quote. Muon supplies a stored-basis close signature, the Operations bot simulates the exact transaction, and that quote's PartyB calls emergencyClosePosition. The dust exception does not require PartyA to act.
  5. Establish the old-basis signature cutoff. After the cleanup signatures are issued, Muon observes RestatementStarted or reads the open restatement state and stops issuing affected old-basis signatures. Operations records the latest possible timestamp among the cleanup signatures and every other affected payload.
  6. Check the frozen book. Before changing any quote, Operations reads every pending and open quote for the symbol. If amount dust appeared after the earlier scan, repeat the PartyB cleanup and establish a new cutoff that includes the later close signature. If any other quote cannot be cancelled, closed, or rewritten, Operations calls abortRestatement while abort is still available. If funding checkpoints exist, the bot then calls processRestatementFunding with the checkpointed PartyB addresses until RestatementAborted is emitted.
  7. Simulate every rewrite batch. From the address that will submit the transaction, the Operations bot uses eth_call to simulate each exact applyAdjustment(symbolId, quoteIds) batch. This runs authorization, quote-status, epoch, liquidation, funding-settlement, conversion, and aggregate-accounting checks without saving any changes. If a batch reverts, Operations resolves the cause or aborts before the first mutation. Simulation does not reserve state, so the bot repeats it immediately before submitting the batch and whenever the caller, IDs, or chain state changes.
  8. Convert the book. Operations calls cancelPendingQuotes for pending inventory and applyAdjustment in batches for eligible open quotes.
  9. Verify coverage. Operations reads the book again and confirms that every affected quote was cancelled, closed, or rewritten.
  10. Finalize, then restore funding in batches. After the last possible old-basis Muon signature has expired and the contract's time gate has passed, Operations calls finalizeRestatement. If checkpoints exist, this begins FINALIZATION_FUNDING_RESTORATION; it does not yet unfreeze the symbol. The bot simulates and submits processRestatementFunding with the checkpointed PartyB addresses until RestatementFinalized is emitted. Only that terminal batch advances basisVersion and unfreezes the symbol. With no checkpoints, finalizeRestatement completes those terminal changes itself.
Do not finalize while an old-basis Muon signature may still be valid. Core knows when the restatement window started, but it cannot verify when Muon observed the window and stopped signing. The Operations bot must track that off-chain. For example, if Muon could issue its last old-basis signature at 12:00 and the signature remains valid for 60 seconds, do not call finalizeRestatement until after 12:01. If Muon stopped after restatementStartedAt, Operations must wait longer than the contract's minimum timer.

finalizeRestatement records one funding-resumption timestamp. Every restoration batch uses that same timestamp. The terminal transaction writes state APPLIED only when the prior state was SCHEDULED or PRICE_ADJUSTED, sets cumulativeFactor to an explicit 1e18, closes the window, clears the temporary funding fields, and increments basisVersion. It retains the latest per-step factor and effectiveTimestamp.

What physical restatement changes

Eligibility, authorization, and batch behavior

applyAdjustment(symbolId, quoteIds) requires an open window. A manager may process any quote. A PartyB may process only its own quote, and authorization is checked separately for every ID. Each quote must match the symbol, have an epoch older than the current window, and have status OPENED, CLOSE_PENDING, or CANCEL_CLOSE_PENDING. The call reverts if PartyA is being liquidated, or if PartyB is in isolated liquidation against that PartyA or in cross liquidation.

Clear every liquidation before the first restatement mutation. Operations must check the PartyA liquidation flag, the PartyB-against-PartyA liquidation flag, and the PartyB cross-liquidation flag before the effective time and again before the first batch.
  • PartyA liquidation: let the normal liquidation finish. Its position-processing step can run while the symbol is frozen only if the required liquidation prices or snapshots were stored before the freeze. If the liquidation is disputed, an account with DISPUTE_ROLE must resolve the settlement amounts before settlement can clear the liquidation flag. The Clearing House can take over a stuck PartyA liquidation, but its position-close path rejects a frozen symbol. Do not start a takeover while an affected open position remains on the frozen symbol.
  • PartyB liquidation: finish isolated and cross PartyB liquidations before the effective freeze. Their position-close paths reject a frozen symbol. If the freeze is already active, use the adjusted-price route to unfreeze only after Muon and PartyB are ready, finish the liquidation, and then reconsider physical restatement. If a mutation-free restatement window is open, abort it first. An abort from effective SCHEDULED remains frozen until Operations confirms the adjusted-price route.
Once a quote rewrite or pending removal has set restatementMutated, Core cannot abort or roll back the window. If a liquidation blocker is found at that point, stop and escalate the incident. Do not finalize an improvised partial conversion.

The call is atomic. Any invalid ID, failed funding charge, arithmetic failure, aggregate overflow, or downstream revert rolls back every earlier quote in the batch. A duplicate epoch reverts with Already restated. Repeating a completed ID reverts, so retries must omit completed IDs. An empty array is accepted and changes nothing.

Conversion arithmetic and fields

previewQuoteAdjustment(symbolId, quoteId) is a read-only preflight helper. It selects the applicable adjustment factor and returns the converted quote fields that applyAdjustment would write. It uses the same arithmetic and underflow checks as the write path, but it does not check quote status, liquidation state, authorization, or whether funding settlement will succeed.

Core converts each amount-and-price pair in two steps. It first scales the amount, then derives the new price from the old notional. This keeps amount * price as close as integer rounding permits. For example, a 4x factor converts 5 units at a price of 20 into 20 units at a price of 5. The notional remains 100. 

adjustedAmount = floor(originalAmount * factor / 1e18)

if originalPrice == 0:
    adjustedPrice = 0
else:
    adjustedPrice = floor(originalAmount * originalPrice / adjustedAmount)

Factors and all amount and price fields use 18 decimals. Math.mulDiv performs each calculation and rounds down.

Part of the quote What Core changes
Entire position Scales quantity. Recalculates openedPrice, initialOpenedPrice, requestedOpenPrice, and marketPrice against the new quantity so each recorded amount-and-price value stays as close as possible to its previous notional.
Already-closed portion When closedAmount is nonzero, scales it and recalculates avgClosedPrice to preserve the recorded closed notional as closely as rounding permits.
Outstanding close request When quantityToClose is nonzero, scales it and recalculates requestedClosePrice to preserve the requested close notional as closely as rounding permits.

The conversion assigns exactly those nine fields. It does not assign IDs, parties, symbol, position or order type, whitelists, statuses, deadlines, parent links, locked values, fee fields, affiliate, funding limit, close fee, or data. Funding settlement runs before conversion on every successful applyAdjustment. It sets lastFundingPaymentTimestamp to the current block time and may change accumulatedPaidFunding and party balances. These are settlement side effects, not part of the nine-field unit conversion.

A reverse split can make a very small raw amount disappear when multiplication is rounded down. The examples below use a 0.01x factor. The numbers are raw 18-decimal amount integers, so one unit in these examples means one amount wei.

  • The entire position would disappear. A quantity of 99 becomes floor(99 * 0.01) = 0. Core rejects the conversion instead of storing a zero-quantity position.
  • Recorded closed quantity would disappear. A nonzero closedAmount of 1 becomes floor(1 * 0.01) = 0. Core rejects the conversion instead of erasing the closed history.
  • The remaining open quantity would disappear. A quote with quantity = 101 and closedAmount = 100 has one amount wei still open. After conversion, both values become 1, leaving no open amount. Core requires the new quantity to remain greater than the new closed amount.
  • A pending close request would disappear. A nonzero quantityToClose of 1 becomes 0. Core rejects the conversion instead of retaining a close request for zero quantity.
PartyB closes an unrestatable quote; Operations does not wait for PartyA.

When the applicable factor would erase total quantity, recorded closed quantity, remaining open quantity, or a pending close amount, emergencyClosePosition permits the quote's own PartyB to close the entire remaining position. The exception works during the scheduled freeze and during an open restatement window. It does not apply while a future schedule is still live. Core does not round or rewrite the quote. A successful close removes it from the live book and leaves the closed quote in its old stored basis as history.

  1. Muon supplies the closing price and both UPNLs in the quote's current stored basis. Core does not apply the adjustment factor to those signature values.
  2. From that quote's PartyB address, the Operations bot simulates the exact emergencyClosePosition call with eth_call, then submits the same call if the simulation succeeds.
  3. Operations verifies that the quote is CLOSED and removes it from the physical-restatement manifest.

A close signature issued after RestatementStarted is a new old-basis signature. Operations must move the recorded old-basis cutoff forward to include its timestamp and delay finalization until it expires. The ordinary liquidation, solvency, funding, pause, signature, and hook checks still run. A non-dust quote remains blocked while the symbol is frozen. After the symbol unfreezes, that quote still needs one of the normal emergency-close conditions.

Price rounding is different: it does not reject every remainder. Core divides the old notional by the adjusted amount and rounds the price down. The discarded remainder is the notional dust. For example: 

old notional   = 3 * 10 = 30
adjusted amount = 4
adjusted price  = floor(30 / 4) = 7
new notional    = 4 * 7 = 28
dust            = 30 - 28 = 2

The dust is simply the remainder from integer division: oldNotional % adjustedAmount. It is always zero or positive and always smaller than adjustedAmount.

Funding settlement and aggregate updates

For each quote, the facet currently executes this order:

  1. Prepare the quote's PartyB funding pair if the window did not checkpoint it.
  2. Charge the quote's accumulated funding on the old basis.
  3. Remove old open amount and opened-price notional from PartyB global, PartyB per-PartyA, and PartyA per-PartyB position aggregates. The helper also subtracts the old weighted-funding contribution.
  4. Subtract the old weighted-funding contribution a second time in the facet, as described below.
  5. Rewrite the nine unit-bearing quote fields.
  6. Add the new position aggregates and one new weighted-funding contribution.
  7. Stamp quoteRestatedEpoch, set the mutation flag, and emit QuoteAdjusted.
The v0.8.6 write path subtracts weighted funding twice. LibQuote.subFromPartiesAggregatedPositions already calls LibAggregateFunding.subFromPartiesAggregateFunding. _restateQuote then calls the same subtraction explicitly before adding the new contribution once. For an old contribution C and new contribution C', the stored result changes by -2C + C', not -C + C'. Do not claim weighted aggregate funding preservation from the current source or existing position-aggregate tests. A physical-restatement procedure must account for this implementation defect before it is used on funded quotes.

Pending inventory

cancelPendingQuotes is manager-only. It does not require an open restatement window, but every quote's symbol must be frozen. It accepts PENDING, LOCKED, and CANCEL_PENDING. The cancellation path does not check quote deadlines. It still rejects the call if PartyA is liquidating or PartyB is liquidating against that PartyA. Core refunds the open trading fee, releases pending locks and PartyB connections where applicable, removes pending indexes, and sets the quote to CANCELED. It then calls cancellation hooks. A hook revert rolls back the batch. An empty quote array is accepted and changes nothing.

Removing a quote from PartyA's pending index records a mutation if a restatement window is open. This applies to cancelPendingQuotes and to normal user-driven pending removal. A cancellation while the symbol is merely frozen, before a window opens, does not mark a future window. Operations must monitor normal cancellation events during a window because the first such removal also makes abort unavailable. Close-request creation, cancellation, and expiry can also change the quote inventory during the window, but those paths do not set the restatement mutation flag.

Mixed-book reads

Raw storage is mixed while batches run. getQuote returns that storage unchanged. getQuoteInVenueUnits and getQuotesInVenueUnits return converted memory copies that can support display, valuation, reconciliation, and hedging when the selected factor represents the intended venue basis. They must not be sent back into Core execution.

Quote condition factorApplied in venue-unit view
Restated in the current open epoch 1e18
Not yet restated in the current open epoch restatementFactor
No open window Active cumulative factor

During the gap after a scheduled adjustment becomes effective but before confirmation or startRestatement, no window is open and these views still apply only the active factor. They ignore the scheduled prospective factor. Their result is therefore not normalized to the post-event venue basis during this gap. To normalize it, convert raw getQuote storage once with getProspectiveCumulativeFactor. Do not apply the full prospective factor to a venue-unit view that already includes an older active factor. Otherwise, suppress affected output until Operations confirms a route transition.

The views do not filter by quote status. When the selected factor is not 1e18, they use the same conversion and underflow checks as preview. A fully closed quote reverts because its converted quantity is not greater than its converted closed amount; amount-rounding underflows can also revert. The array getter is atomic, so one non-convertible ID reverts the entire batch. Use bounded batches with a single-ID fallback, and use raw getQuote for closed history or any record that cannot be converted.

The venue-unit view also returns the current epoch, freeze status, and storedInVenueUnits. That last flag means only that this view applied 1e18. It is not independent proof of a quote's provenance or coverage.

Funding across the window

A physical restatement changes quote quantities. Current funding rates are per-unit values, so the same rate cannot be used with old-unit and new-unit quantities. Operations first supplies every involved PartyB. Core snapshots that PartyB's total LONG and SHORT open quantities and, when accumulated funding is active, saves and pauses its current rates. Preparation is then sealed: quote processing cannot introduce a previously omitted PartyB. Before the window closes, Core requires every snapshotted old-basis quantity to be consumed and restores every saved rate. It rebases rates only when the restatement is finalized. 

open window → snapshot PartyB inventory and funding → seal manifest → resolve old-basis quantities → restore funding → close window

When the window opens

startRestatement records one fundingCutoffTimestamp. It does not read or loop over MAStorage.partyBList, and it does not change any PartyB's rates. Core always enters FUNDING_PREPARATION so Operations can submit the complete involved-PartyB manifest. When accumulated funding is inactive, funding checkpoint creation is skipped, but the open-position inventory snapshots are still required.

Prepare PartyB funding pairs

The manager calls processRestatementFunding(symbolId, partyBs) with a gas-bounded address array. Core examines only those addresses. For every new address in the epoch, it snapshots the PartyB's current total LONG and SHORT open quantity for the symbol. Repeated addresses are idempotent. Core does not enumerate the global PartyB registry, so the manager remains responsible for supplying every involved PartyB before sealing preparation.

For each supplied PartyB, Core does one of the following:

  • skips a checkpoint already created for the current restatement epoch;
  • skips a symbol pair whose epochDuration is zero;
  • skips a pair whose current long and short rates are both zero; or
  • rolls accumulated funding to fundingCutoffTimestamp, saves both current rates, increments pendingFundingPartyBCount, and sets both current rates to zero.

A pair with one zero rate and one nonzero rate is checkpointed. Repeated addresses are harmless. Current registry membership is not checked, so Operations may submit registered or deregistered PartyBs. Registering or deregistering a PartyB during the window does not change funding progress.

Keep and seal the PartyB address list. Core saves inventory and funding checkpoints under each submitted PartyB address. It can report counts and per-PartyB inventory progress, but it cannot return all addresses or prove that an entire PartyB was omitted. Operations must therefore save and independently reconcile the complete submitted manifest. When aborting or finalizing, submit every PartyB with a funding checkpoint again through processRestatementFunding until the funding pending count reaches zero. Core safely skips unknown or already-restored addresses. If Operations loses the address of a live checkpoint, Core cannot restore that rate and the symbol remains frozen. Before sending a batch, simulate the exact call from the manager address.

After Operations has submitted and checked the intended set, the manager calls completeRestatementFundingPreparation. Core then enters QUOTE_PROCESSING and rejects later PartyB preparation. The seal is an attestation that the submitted address set is complete. Within that set, completeness is enforced on-chain: finalization requires every snapshotted LONG and SHORT quantity to reach zero.

While quotes are being rewritten

A checkpointed pair keeps its current long and short rates at zero. Funding accumulated before the window remains recorded at the fixed cutoff. Later batches use that same cutoff even if they run in later blocks.

Each successful rewrite subtracts the quote's old quantity from the matching PartyB LONG or SHORT inventory checkpoint. Ordinary and emergency full closes use the same accounting hook, so removing an old-basis position also consumes its checkpoint quantity. A quote belonging to a PartyB that was not prepared before the seal is rejected. Pending quotes are not part of this open-position completeness counter; their cancellation remains a separate operational workflow.

Restore rates before closing the window

abortRestatement and finalizeRestatement stop quote processing and start a restoration phase. They report the pending checkpoint count. If the count is zero, the initiating transaction closes the window immediately. Otherwise, Operations submits the saved PartyB manifest through processRestatementFunding in gas-bounded batches.

Abort Not rolled into accumulated funding. Accounting resumes as if the saved rates had remained active. The saved long and short rates are restored unchanged.

newRate = sign(oldRate) * floor(abs(oldRate) * 1e18 / restatementFactor)

Flooring may reduce the restored magnitude. Core rejects a magnitude that cannot fit in int256. Each restoration batch ignores unknown or repeated addresses, deletes every matching current-epoch checkpoint, and decrements pendingFundingPartyBCount. The transaction that reduces the count to zero closes the window and emits RestatementAborted or RestatementFinalized.

Enforced and operational boundaries

What the freeze enforces

The freeze is not a global symbol pause. Internal requireNotFrozen checks block paths that resolve the affected symbol. In v0.8.6 those checks cover:

  • quote creation, PartyB open and close execution, batch wrappers, and solver-fee wrappers;
  • legacy and unified settlement;
  • force actions, three-step force-close finalization, non-dust emergency PartyB actions, clearing-house closes, and ADL-style closes;
  • PartyA liquidation price snapshot setup in new and legacy paths, and PartyB position liquidation;
  • funding charge, rate setting, epoch-duration changes, and accumulated-funding paths.

emergencyClosePosition has one narrow freeze exception. If the applicable physical factor would make an unrestated quote fail an amount-underflow check, the quote's PartyB may fully close it with a stored-basis Muon signature. Core does not treat a quote already rewritten in the current epoch as dust by applying the factor a second time.

The freeze leaves several quote mutations callable. PartyA can call requestToClosePosition, which changes an open quote to CLOSE_PENDING and writes the requested close price, close quantity, order type, deadline, and modification timestamp. PartyA can cancel a close request, and anyone can pass an expired close request to expireQuote; neither path checks the symbol freeze. The frozen manifest is therefore not static. Operations must monitor and reconcile close-request creation, cancellation, and expiry throughout batching.

Other non-price cleanup can remain callable. A function that neither receives a symbol nor resolves one from a quote cannot apply the freeze check. The freeze also does not cancel an existing liquidation. Normal PartyA position processing has no symbol-freeze check, but its price and snapshot setup does. PartyB liquidation closes and Clearing House liquidation closes do check the freeze. This is why Operations must clear liquidation blockers before the first restatement mutation.

Signature expiry and account-level payloads

Muon payloads do not carry basisVersion. Core uses a time gate, rather than a payload version, to separate old and new bases. Finalization requires: 

block.timestamp > restatementStartedAt + LibMuon.maxUpnlValidTime()

Signature verification accepts the exact expiry boundary and rejects only after it. Finalization uses the strict greater-than check, so equality still fails. A zero per-function validity override falls back to the global validity. maxUpnlValidTime() takes the maximum of the global setting and every enum override, using the live configuration when finalization runs.

For direct restatement, restatementStartedAt is the later of effectiveTimestamp and adjustmentScheduledAt. This prevents a past-effective emergency schedule from backdating the wait before its scheduling transaction, but it does not prove that off-chain issuance stopped then. For a later restatement opened while the symbol is live, restatementStartedAt is the block time of startRestatement.

Account-level UPNL payloads do not list every symbol in the account. Core cannot infer that such a payload includes exposure to the frozen symbol, so Muon must stop issuing affected account-level signatures off-chain. The cutoff must cover every affected account-, pair-, and quote-level payload. Record the latest old-basis signature timestamp, or prove an upper bound from the issuance cutoff and allowed signer clock skew. Core checks each signature's timestamp and does not reject a future timestamp.

The on-chain finalization deadline is sufficient only when the proven latest old-basis timestamp is no later than restatementStartedAt. Otherwise Operations must wait until the block time is strictly greater than the latest old-basis timestamp bound plus the maximum applicable validity, even if finalizeRestatement would already succeed. A delayed cutoff is not detected by successful finalization.

Governance and Operations must also prevent a later validity increase from reviving an old-basis signature. Before applying a new global or per-function validity, require the current block time to be strictly greater than the latest timestamp bound for the affected old-basis signatures plus the proposed applicable validity. The recorded freeze start is a safe substitute only when the cutoff proof shows that no old-basis timestamp can be later than that start.

basisVersion and deferred force close

Finalization increments basisVersion, even if no quote was processed. The three-step force-close flow records this value at initialization. Its refresh step validates a fresh signature but deliberately does not replace the stored close price or basis version. Finalization of that force close requires the symbol to be unfrozen and the stored version to match, so an old-basis workflow must be initialized again. This protection is specific to that three-step flow. It is not a generic field in Muon signatures or every deferred workflow.

Revert-worthy boundaries

  • Schedule rejects an unknown symbol, a factor outside [1e16, 100e18], factor 1e18, an open window, or another SCHEDULED step.
  • Any call that computes a prospective factor can revert if the quotient exceeds uint256. Confirm and direct start also reject a call before effectiveTimestamp and reject a prospective factor of zero.
  • Start rejects an existing window and normalized factor 1e18.
  • Abort rejects no window or any window whose mutation flag is true.
  • Apply rejects authorization, symbol, epoch, status, and liquidation failures, plus funding, arithmetic, or aggregate failures reached downstream.
  • The dust emergency-close exception requires an unrestated quote that fails an amount conversion. PartyB ownership, liquidation, pause, status, signature, solvency, settlement, and hook checks still apply.
  • Pending cancellation rejects an unfrozen symbol, an ineligible status, liquidation guards, or a reverting hook.
  • Finalize rejects no window, missing preparation attestation, and the exact or earlier signature-expiry boundary. A funding batch rejects an empty PartyB array, no open window, an invalid phase, or signed-rate overflow. Unknown, repeated, and already processed addresses are skipped.

Operations runbook

Upgrade the close-accounting graph atomically. The inventory-consumption hook is compiled into LibQuoteClose, ClearingHouseFacetImpl, LibPartyALiquidationProcess, PartyBLiquidationFacet, and SymbolAdjustmentFacet. Deploy the complete linked core graph from a fresh upgrade state, then replace every facet linked to those new libraries in one Diamond cut. This includes the PartyA, PartyB position/quote/batch/emergency, force-action/force-close, PartyA liquidation/snapshot, PartyB liquidation, ClearingHouse, Symbol Adjustment, and ViewFacetSymbol facets. The view facet receives every Symbol Adjustment read selector in the same cut. Replacing only SymbolAdjustmentFacet leaves close paths that do not consume the new inventory counter and can freeze finalization. Do not run the one-facet updater for either facet: selector ownership moves across both ABIs and must be reconciled from the complete target selector map. Before the cut, verify that no symbol has an open restatement window: a window started under the older implementation has no inventory snapshots to migrate. Do not open a new window until the selector and runtime code-hash checks for the full cut are complete.

Prepare and schedule

  1. Verify the venue notice, symbol ID, factor direction, effective time, and allowed factor range.
  2. Read the active factor and compute the intended prospective product before scheduling. Require 0 < floor(activeFactor * intendedFactor / 1e18) <= type(uint256).max. Do not infer the new basis from only the latest step.
  3. Build one open and pending quote manifest across all PartyB account models. For every pair, require PartyA liquidation, PartyB-against-PartyA liquidation, and PartyB cross-liquidation status to be clear. Resolve any active liquidation before the effective time.
  4. Build the complete involved-PartyB manifest from the affected open book and Operations' funding records. Fork-rehearse inventory and funding preparation, quote processing, and restoration with the intended address batches. Choose a batch size with comfortable gas headroom and test bot restart from the saved manifest.
  5. Resolve the duplicate weighted-funding subtraction risk before approving any physical route on funded inventory.
  6. Verify the deployed ABI exposes startRestatement(uint256), processRestatementFunding(uint256,address[]), completeRestatementFundingPreparation(uint256), getRestatementFundingProgress(uint256), and getRestatementInventoryProgress(uint256,address). All Symbol Adjustment read selectors are routed through ViewFacetSymbol; SymbolAdjustmentFacet exposes mutation selectors only. The old three-argument start selector is not exposed.
  7. Call scheduleAdjustment. Record the transaction block time, adjustment index, factor, effective time, prospective factor, and current maximum Muon validity. Confirm that getProspectiveCumulativeFactor returns the intended product without reverting.
  8. Run previewQuoteAdjustment for every eligible open quote now that the schedule supplies the prospective factor. Treat this as an arithmetic preview, not an eligibility or funding-settlement proof. Mark every amount-underflow failure for PartyB cleanup when the symbol reaches its effective freeze.

Reach the effective boundary

  1. Refresh the manifest before the effective time. Trading is still live for a future schedule.
  2. For a future schedule, stop ordinary old-basis account-, pair-, and quote-level issuance before the effective time. For a past-effective emergency, stop ordinary issuance before submitting the schedule transaction. Do not establish the final old-basis cutoff yet: dust cleanup may require dedicated close signatures after the symbol freezes.
  3. At equality or later, verify isSymbolFrozen and the raw venue feed basis.
  4. If the event did not occur, call cancelAdjustment. Verify the symbol unfreezes and the older active factor remains unchanged. End this runbook here; do not run dust cleanup for a called-off event.

Use the adjusted-price route

  1. Have Muon compute old-unit prices from getProspectiveCumulativeFactor.
  2. Compare independent calculations with the feed and running notional and UPNL checks.
  3. Call confirmPriceAdjusted. Verify PRICE_ADJUSTED, the expected active factor, and unfreeze.
  4. Resume Muon signatures and PartyB funding inputs in the adjusted old-unit basis.

Use either physical-restatement route

  1. Keep ordinary affected signatures stopped. Call startRestatement(symbolId) and record epoch, factor, start time, funding cutoff, phase, pending funding count, and the zeroed symbol-wide LONG and SHORT inventory totals. Do not expect this call to emit checkpoint events, inspect the PartyB registry, snapshot quantities, or zero any rates.
  2. While progress reports FUNDING_PREPARATION, simulate and submit processRestatementFunding(symbolId, partyBs) from the manager address for each bounded manifest batch. Verify inventory snapshots with events and getRestatementInventoryProgress; when funding is active, also verify funding checkpoint creation with events, the pending count, or isRestatementFundingCheckpointed. After the complete involved-PartyB set has been supplied and reconciled, call completeRestatementFundingPreparation. The manifest is sealed at that transition; do not submit quote rewrites before progress reports QUOTE_PROCESSING.
  3. Re-scan the now-frozen book before the first mutation. Recheck statuses, every liquidation mode, previews, funding settlement, hooks, and all deregistered PartyBs with open quotes. For every marked or newly discovered amount-underflow quote, have PartyB obtain a stored-basis Muon signature. Simulate the exact emergencyClosePosition call from the PartyB address, submit it, and verify the quote is CLOSED. PartyA does not participate.
  4. After cleanup, record the latest possible timestamp across every old-basis payload, including the dedicated close signatures. Add allowed signer clock skew and prove that Muon cannot issue a later old-basis timestamp. This is the cutoff used for finalization.
  5. If any item cannot be processed, abort before a quote rewrite or pending removal. Direct abort stays frozen in SCHEDULED. Active-factor abort preserves the prior state, normally live PRICE_ADJUSTED, but it can also return live in CANCELLED. After abortRestatement, submit the checkpointed PartyB manifest through processRestatementFunding until RestatementAborted if progress reports ABORT_FUNDING_RESTORATION.
  6. Cancel pending quotes as the operational workflow requires. Pending quotes are not included in the open-position inventory counter. Monitor normal pending cancellation and close-request creation, cancellation, and expiry throughout the window, and reconcile each resulting status and price-bearing close field before processing the quote.
  7. Before submitting each bounded batch, have the Operations bot simulate the exact applyAdjustment(symbolId, quoteIds) call through eth_call from the intended transaction sender. Submit only a successful simulation, then track QuoteAdjusted and verify each quote's epoch. Remove successful IDs before retrying. Re-simulate whenever the batch or relevant state changes.
  8. Use venue-unit views for convertible records during the open window, but compare raw storage and epoch data to prove physical coverage. Keep batches bounded, retry failed batches one ID at a time, and use raw getQuote for closed or non-convertible history.
  9. Re-scan the complete manifest. For every submitted PartyB, confirm getRestatementInventoryProgress reports zero remaining LONG and SHORT quantity for that PartyB, and confirm both symbol-wide remaining quantity totals are zero. Independently verify that no involved PartyB was omitted. Reconcile position aggregates, funding checkpoints, and weighted aggregate funding explicitly.
  10. Lock Muon validity configuration. Prove that the old-basis timestamp upper bound is no later than restatementStartedAt. If it is later, extend the operational deadline to that upper bound plus the maximum applicable validity. Wait strictly past both that deadline and the on-chain restatementStartedAt + maxUpnlValidTime() deadline.
  11. Call finalizeRestatement. If progress reports FINALIZATION_FUNDING_RESTORATION, simulate and submit the checkpointed PartyB manifest in bounded processRestatementFunding batches until RestatementFinalized. A nonzero inventory pending count makes the initial finalization call revert. Do not treat the initial transaction's success as lifecycle completion, signature-expiry proof, or proof that no PartyB was omitted.
  12. Verify the expected lifecycle state, explicit 1e18 cumulative factor, closed window, incremented basisVersion, phase NONE, zero pending checkpoint count, deleted checkpoints, rebased rates, and unfreeze.
  13. Resume Muon with raw venue prices. Reinitialize stale three-step force closes and retain the old-basis timestamp cutoff proof. Before any later validity increase, wait strictly past that timestamp bound plus the proposed applicable validity.

NONE = 0 No schedule has been stored. A manager may schedule.

Phase Meaning and operator action
NONE = 0 No funding work is pending. Outside a window, this is the terminal phase.

Storage retains only the latest schedule, so indexers need these events for complete adjustment history. Preparation events distinguish PartyB address counts from LONG and SHORT position quantities, report each fresh inventory snapshot, summarize every batch, and record the exact quantities sealed for quote processing. Each inventory-consumption event identifies the quote, side, and old-basis quantity consumed. Starting from the per-PartyB snapshots, indexers subtract these side-specific deltas to reconstruct the same exact PartyB and symbol remainders enforced on-chain, without a second completion log. Funding-restoration events report restoration mode and remaining PartyB work. Per-pair preparation and restoration also emit the existing AccumulatedFundingStateUpdated. When checkpoints exist, RestatementAborted or RestatementFinalized is emitted by the last funding batch rather than the transaction that starts restoration. Every successful applyAdjustment emits QuoteFundingSettled before QuoteAdjusted, including when the settled funding amount is zero. Indexers need both events to attribute the settlement side effects and the later unit conversion.
