Close Settlement Netting

v0.8.6 admits a close against the final bilateral difference between accumulated funding and realized PnL, instead of requiring each gross leg to be independently funded in execution order. Batch closes also reject repeated quote IDs before mutation and finalize every quote before the first hook runs.

Summary

A quote close can contain two bilateral obligations: accumulated funding and realized PnL. Either component can make PartyA pay PartyB or PartyB pay PartyA. Previously, the close transferred funding first and PnL second. Each transfer checked the payer's balance in isolation, so a party could fail the first gross debit even when the second component would immediately credit more than enough to cover it.

The close path now records both obligations before moving allocated balances, calculates the final PartyA-to-PartyB difference, checks only that difference, credits receivers, and then debits payers. Funding and PnL remain separate in the event ledger; only admission is netted.

Core rule. A party must cover what it owes after funding and realized PnL offset each other, not the largest temporary debit created by contract execution order.

Before: gross-leg admission

The previous close body interleaved calculation, transfer, state finalization, and external observation:

1. Calculate and immediately transfer accumulated funding
2. Calculate and immediately transfer realized PnL
3. Charge PartyA's platform close fee
4. Update closed amount, locks, aggregate positions, and quote status
5. Call close hooks

Changing the order to PnL-first would only reverse which sign combination could fail. The defect was not that funding came first; it was that balance admission happened independently for components of one atomic bilateral settlement.

The 70 / 80 / 90 failure

Consider a full close where:

  • PartyB starts with 70 allocated.
  • Funding is -80, so PartyB owes PartyA 80.
  • PartyA realizes a 90 loss, so PartyA owes PartyB 90.
The close is net-negative for PartyA even though its funding component is positive.
PartyA perspective Amount
Receives funding +80
Pays realized loss -90
Final bilateral result PartyA pays 10

The old path tried to debit 80 from PartyB while PartyB held only 70, then reverted before the 90 PnL credit. The valid final PartyB balance is 70 + 90 - 80 = 80. Only PartyA needs to cover the net 10, plus its separately charged close fee.

Settlement model

Funding and PnL are converted into unsigned PartyA-side receive and pay totals.
Input Meaning Bucket
fundingFee > 0 PartyA pays PartyB partyAPays
fundingFee < 0 PartyA receives from PartyB partyAReceives
PartyA profit PartyB pays realized PnL partyAReceives
PartyA loss PartyA pays realized PnL partyAPays 
partyAReceives =
    (PartyA made profit ? pnl : 0)
  + (fundingFee < 0 ? abs(fundingFee) : 0)

partyAPays =
    (PartyA made loss ? pnl : 0)
  + (fundingFee > 0 ? fundingFee : 0)

netToPartyA = partyAReceives - partyAPays
  • If netToPartyA > 0, PartyB must cover that difference.
  • If netToPartyA < 0, PartyA must cover its absolute value.
  • If it is zero, neither party needs a temporary bilateral settlement buffer.

For a batch, both buckets are summed across every quote before either party's allocated balance is checked. The PartyB side uses the PartyA-specific allocation key shared by all quotes in that batch.

Single-quote close

  1. Prepare. Validate proportional lock-release floors, reduce quote and global locks, record accumulated funding without transferring it, calculate PnL, and return the complete settlement obligations.
  2. Net-check. Derive partyAReceives and partyAPays, then require only the final debtor to cover the difference.
  3. Credit. Credit the funding receiver and the realized-PnL receiver.
  4. Debit. Debit the funding payer and the realized-PnL payer.
  5. Finalize. Emit funding attribution, charge PartyA's close fee, credit the fee collector, update average closed price, closed amount, aggregate positions, requested quantity, status, and connections.
  6. Publish. Invoke close and fee hooks from final quote state, then emit trade volume.
Why credits may come first. The net check has already proved that the party with the final debit can cover the difference. Any later debit, fee, finalization, or hook failure reverts the complete transaction, including the earlier credits, lock release, and funding checkpoint.

Batch close

The PartyB batch entry retains its length checks, Muon verification, and signed post-close solvency boundary. It then hands the complete batch to LibQuoteClose.closeQuotes, which runs one coordinated pipeline:

  1. Reject duplicate quote IDs before counters, funding checkpoints, or locks mutate.
  2. Apply existing liquidation gates and increment both parties' UPNL counters.
  3. Validate each fill and enforce one PartyA, PartyB, signer, and allocation relationship.
  4. Prepare every quote and aggregate every funding and realized-PnL obligation.
  5. Check the single batch-wide bilateral difference.
  6. Credit every receiver across the batch.
  7. Debit every payer across the batch.
  8. Finalize every quote and retain each calculated close fee.
  9. Call hooks for every quote.
  10. Return final statuses and close IDs to the external facet.

This makes offsetting funding and PnL independent of quote input order. The standalone accumulated-funding batch uses the same posting discipline: record each execution-time fee, credit every receiver, debit every payer, emit funding settlement events, then run the existing signed-time solvency calculations.

Signed time and execution time remain distinct. The Muon UPNL already contains funding debt at the signature timestamp. Solvency removes that signed amount, while allocated balances receive the amount calculated when the transaction executes.

Why duplicate quote IDs are rejected

Batch preparation runs before closedAmount is finalized. Repeating one quote would make later entries read a stale open amount while shrinking locks that an earlier entry already reduced.

For the default quote, a repeated [quoteId, quoteId] batch with 40% and 60% fills would produce this sequence:

The stale second denominator closes the quote while leaving real collateral locked.
Lock Start After correct 40% Incorrect residue after 60%
CVA 22 13.2 5.28
LF 3 1.8 0.72
PartyA MM 75 45 18
PartyB MM 40 24 9.6

The quote could reach closedAmount == quantity and CLOSED while those amounts remained in the quote and both parties' global locked totals. v0.8.6 therefore rejects duplicate quote IDs with LibQuoteClose: Duplicate quoteId before any mutation. Multiple sequential fills of one quote require separate transactions.

The uniqueness preflight is an in-memory O(n²) scan. It avoids storage and preserves the no-mutation failure boundary. Correctness is covered, but a maximum-batch gas benchmark has not been added.

Hook compatibility contract

Custom hooks can query quote and account state, so callback timing is an external compatibility surface.

The final design rejects the intermediate mixed snapshot in favor of one coherent batch state.
Behavior State visible to the first close hook
Original sequential batch Quote 1 was final; quote 2 had not been settled or finalized.
Rejected intermediate design All balances and funding were posted, but later quotes still had old closed amount and status.
v0.8.6 contract Every balance, fee, lock, closed amount, aggregate, and status in the batch is final.

Per quote, callbacks retain this order:

  1. Affiliate onClosePosition.
  2. Affiliate onFeeCharged(CLOSE).
  3. System onClosePosition.
  4. System onFeeCharged(CLOSE).
  5. TradeVolumeRecorded(CLOSE).

LibHook.safeCall clears signer and InstantLayer privileges before each external call and restores them afterward. Hook failure is not swallowed: it raises HookReverted and atomically reverts the complete close.

Compatibility decision. Custom hooks that relied on quote-by-quote intermediate batch state must adapt. The first callback now observes one fully finalized batch, including both parties' final allocated balances.

Observable event order

Admission is netted, but accounting attribution remains gross. Consumers continue to receive distinct funding-in, funding-out, realized-PnL-in, and realized-PnL-out balance changes.

  1. Credit loop: all receiver-side funding and realized-PnL BalanceChange events, in quote input order.
  2. Debit loop: all payer-side funding and realized-PnL BalanceChange events, in quote input order.
  3. Finalize loop: QuoteFundingSettled, PartyA's PLATFORM_FEE_OUT balance change, TradingFeeCharged(CLOSE), then quote state finalization.
  4. Hook loop: affiliate callbacks, system callbacks, then TradeVolumeRecorded(CLOSE) per quote.
  5. External facet loop: both FillCloseRequest overloads per quote, using final status, close ID, and locked values.

This is intentionally different from the original cross-quote order, where settlement, fees, hooks, and fill events for quote 1 all preceded settlement of quote 2.

Invariants and boundaries

  • Net admission: collateral admission uses the combined funding-plus-PnL difference.
  • Gross attribution: funding and PnL retain separate balance reasons and events.
  • Credit before debit: every applicable receiver credit precedes payer debits.
  • Atomicity: any later failure rolls back funding checkpoints, locks, balances, state, and logs.
  • Unique IDs: a close batch rejects repeated quote IDs before mutation.
  • Common relationship: every batch quote belongs to the same PartyA and PartyB.
  • Final hook snapshot: no close hook runs until all batch quotes are finalized.
  • Lock conservation: a full close clears quote and global locks for both parties.

Close-fee boundary

The bilateral net check covers only accumulated funding and realized PnL between PartyA and PartyB. PartyA's platform close fee is calculated and charged during finalization, after bilateral posting. It is not included in partyAPays. If PartyA cannot pay it, the complete transaction reverts.

Passing the net check is not a complete success guarantee. Price validation, signed solvency gates, close-fee capacity, finalization, and hooks can still revert and roll back the operation.

Non-goals and known limits

  • Funding-rate formulas, Muon verification, signed UPNL formulas, price validation, and fee formulas are unchanged.
  • Sequential same-quote fills inside one batch are not simulated; duplicates are rejected.
  • The duplicate scan has no maximum-size gas benchmark.
  • The dedicated hook observer covers two full closes, not a mixed partial/full batch.
  • Hook failures intentionally revert the parent transaction.
