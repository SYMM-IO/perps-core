Per-Solver Liquidation Overshoot

A manager can let a selected solver close PartyA a small distance below the liquidation boundary, reducing the chance that a tiny favorable market move makes the account solvent again before liquidation starts.

Summary

Normal close fills still require PartyA and PartyB to finish solvent. The two fillCloseRequestToLiquidation paths may use a PartyB- and symbol-specific overshoot. PartyB must remain solvent, while PartyA may finish below zero by no more than the calculated allowance.

The default rate for every PartyB is zero, so the overshoot stays inactive until a manager opts a solver in. The external close selectors are unchanged, and the allowance is never supplied by PartyB. Independently of the overshoot, the fee-aware method quotes its solver fee for maxFillAmount; see Execution behavior for what that requires of callers.

Allowance calculation

postCloseThreshold = remaining account locked CVA + remaining account locked LF
allowedShortfall   = floor(postCloseThreshold * effectiveRate / 1e18)

The threshold is account-level: it includes CVA and LF belonging to other open positions. The closing quote's CVA and LF are released separately with the same integer rounding used by settlement. The selected quantity is then checked against the exact post-close balance before state changes are accepted.

For example, suppose the zero-rate close would leave a $10 threshold. A 5 bps rate is stored as 5e14 and permits at most $10 * 0.0005 = $0.005 of PartyA shortfall. Operations can opt a PartyB into this default with setPartyBLiquidationOvershootRate(partyB, 0, 5e14).

The allowance applies only when closing is harmful to PartyA and PartyA's available balance starts above zero. When closing is beneficial or neutral and a full close still leaves PartyA insolvent, the call reverts, and a PartyA already at or below zero cannot close anything through this path; the overshoot does not extend either rule.

Configuration

  • setPartyBLiquidationOvershootRate(partyB, 0, rate) sets that PartyB's default.
  • A nonzero symbol inherits symbol 0 unless it has an explicit override.
  • Setting a symbol-specific rate to zero explicitly disables a nonzero PartyB default for that symbol.
  • clearPartyBLiquidationOvershootRateOverride restores inheritance from symbol 0.
  • getPartyBLiquidationOvershootRate returns the effective rate and the override state.

Only PARTY_B_MANAGER_ROLE can set or clear rates. The target must be a registered PartyB, and the symbol must be 0 or an existing symbol. Rates use 1e18 precision and are capped at 1e18, so the allowance never exceeds the post-close threshold itself.

Execution behavior

The legacy and fee-aware methods share the normal internal close pipeline: Muon verification, bound-mode handling, solvency, UPNL counters, settlement, hooks, and fill events remain in one execution path. A normal fillCloseRequest always passes an allowance of zero.

The solver quotes its fee for maxFillAmount. For every candidate amount, the fee-aware planner uses maxSolverFee * candidateAmount / maxFillAmount; the final transfer uses the same formula with filledAmount. An undersized maxFillAmount remains a best-effort ceiling and may leave PartyA solvent. Because maxFillAmount is the fee basis, a fee-bearing call must pass a concrete quantity: an effectively unbounded value such as type(uint256).max makes the prorated fee round down to zero, so nothing is charged. Use the quote's remaining close quantity when no tighter cap is wanted.

Remaining-value fallback

An overshoot can increase the fill enough to leave a nonzero remainder below minAcceptableQuoteValue. In that case, the planner falls back to the capped zero-rate amount and validates the remainder again. If that amount is also invalid, the call keeps the existing Remaining quote value is low revert. A full close is valid because its remaining value is zero.

getMaxCloseAmountToLiquidation uses the same fallback. Its canCloseAll result means the full close fits within the configured allowance; it does not mean PartyA necessarily remains solvent.

Integration and events

  • SetPartyBLiquidationOvershootRate records the PartyB, symbol, old and new effective rates, and override state.
  • PartyALiquidationOvershootUsed is emitted only when the calculated final PartyA balance is negative. It reports the effective rate, allowed shortfall, and actual shortfall.
  • The getMaxCloseAmountToLiquidation selector is served by ViewFacetQuote, so an upgrade must move it in the diamond cut. The view validates the pending close request (status, deadline, price, LIMIT order) before computing and reverts for a request execution would reject.

Risk boundary

The allowance is not capped by remaining LF, only by the full post-close CVA + LF at the maximum 1e18 rate. A manager who configures a large rate can therefore intentionally allow a LATE liquidation, changing who absorbs the deficit; a further adverse market move before liquidation can still push it to OVERDUE. Use large values only as an explicit risk decision.

The overshoot close and PartyA liquidation are separate transactions. The close makes PartyA eligible under its signed accounting snapshot, but it cannot guarantee eligibility after a favorable market move larger than the overshoot.
