Liquidation Dispute Resolution Guards

A dispute resolution writes settlement amounts and reserve contributions. It now requires the PartyA to be under liquidation and every listed PartyB to hold a pending settlement bucket.

What a resolution writes

When a PartyA liquidation is disputed, the dispute role calls resolveLiquidationDispute with the PartyBs and the amounts it has verified. For each PartyB the call overwrites the planned actualAmount of its settlement bucket and re-synchronises the PartyB’s liquidation settlement reserve, the amount a cross-mode PartyB keeps unavailable until the settlement is paid (see Cross-PartyB Liquidation Reserve Enforcement). It also records the liquidator action timestamp and sets or clears the disputed flag.

Writes outside a liquidation

None of that was conditional on a liquidation being open, or on the bucket existing. A call for a PartyA that was not liquidated set a disputed flag and an action timestamp on an empty liquidation record. A call naming a PartyB that had no pending bucket created one in all but the pending flag: an actualAmount that no settlement would ever pay, and a reserve contribution that reduced the PartyB’s cross availability until a later liquidation of the same pair overwrote it.

The role is trusted, so this is protection against an operator mistake rather than against an attacker, but the effect of the mistake was a silently inconsistent reserve.

The guards

require(liquidationStatus[partyA], "LiquidationFacet: PartyA is solvent");
require(settlementStates[partyA][partyB].pending, "LiquidationFacet: PartyB is not in settlement");

The first is checked once; the second for every PartyB in the list, before anything is written. A resolution that lists an already-settled PartyB therefore reverts as a whole, matching what settlePartyALiquidation does with the same input. Amounts and the disputed flag are otherwise written exactly as before, and the planned amounts of unlisted PartyBs are untouched.
