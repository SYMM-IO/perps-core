Zero-Quantity Quote Rejection

A market quote with zero quantity passed every check and opened a position with no notional. Nothing could close it, and a liquidation of its owner could never finish. Quantity is now checked at entry, and stored positions of that kind can be cleared.

How a zero-quantity position came to exist

sendQuote validated the symbol, the deadline, the locked values against the symbol’s minimum quote value and the leverage, but never the quantity itself. A quote could carry normal locked values and a quantity of zero. On the open side, a MARKET quote has to be filled in full, and the check for that was quantity == filledAmount: zero equals zero, so the fill went through. The leverage check divides notional by locked values and a notional of zero passes it as well.

The result was an OPENED position with locked collateral, an opened price, and no size.

Why it could never leave

Every path that closes a position divides by its open amount:

  • the average closed price, (avgClosedPrice * closedAmount + filled * price) / (closedAmount + filled);
  • the proportional release of locked values on a partial close;
  • the CVA + LF released in the solvency check before a close.

With a zero open amount each of these is a division by zero and reverts with a panic. A regular close, an emergency close and a liquidation close all failed. For a liquidation this is the worst case: the account’s position count never reaches zero, settlement requires it to, and the account stays flagged as liquidated with every counterparty’s settlement bucket pending.

Who could trigger it. Any PartyA could send the quote; a PartyB had to fill it. A PartyB that filled such a quote against its own PartyA could then block that account’s liquidation indefinitely.

Rejection at entry

  1. sendQuote requires quantity > 0 next to the locked-value checks, with PartyAFacet: Zero quantity. All sendQuote wrappers share the check.
  2. openPosition requires a positive fill for MARKET quotes as it already did for LIMIT quotes, with PartyBFacet: Invalid filledAmount.

Clearing stored positions

A position stored before the checks existed must still be able to leave. The close arithmetic now tolerates a zero open amount instead of dividing by it:

  • closePositionFully leaves the average closed price untouched when there is nothing to average.
  • LibQuoteClose releases all of the quote’s locked values, since nothing is proportional, and skips the minimum-fill checks that would divide by the open amount.
  • LibSolvency counts the whole CVA + LF as released by the close.

Such a quote closes at the given price with zero PnL, releases its locks and leaves the position count. It can be cleared by emergencyClosePosition or by liquidation; both reach settlement normally.

Operator notes

After upgrading, scan the deployment for OPENED quotes whose quantity is zero and clear them. No other quote is affected: the average-price rounding for real quotes is unchanged, and the new checks only reject inputs that could never have formed a valid position.
