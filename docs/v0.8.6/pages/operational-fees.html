v0.8.6/Fees

Operational Fees

A fee primitive for per-operation work that should not be embedded in quote pricing.

Summary

v0.8.6 introduces a standing per-(payer, charger) operational-fee allowance. A charger can be a solver (PartyB) or a registered relayer. Each charge draws from the named payer's Symmio collateral balance and counts against that allowance.

The same allowance can carry a simple priority multiplier. 10000 means normal priority; a higher value tells relayers and solvers that this payer is willing to pay above the normal fee when capacity is tight.

The debit draws the payer's free balance first, then allocated margin as fallback. Fees land in the charger's configured receiver account (defaulting to the charger itself). A charge reverts if the resolved receiver is the payer, so a charger cannot route a payer's operational fee back into the same free-balance bucket.

This is separate from the v0.8.6 solver rate fee, which is notional-based, per-quote, and stored in SolverFeeCaps.

Why This Exists

Some service costs are flat per-operation charges rather than notional-based trading fees. Operational fees give those costs a dedicated path, outside open and close price semantics.

Trust Model

The allowance grant is the consent. Core does not require the payer to sign each specific operation or quote; it only checks charged + amount <= allowance[payer][charger] before collecting the fee.

The authority is bounded by four controls:

  1. Registry: registered PartyBs are chargers by default. Non-PartyB services, such as relayers, must be registered by governance (FEE_ADMIN_ROLE) before they can charge.
  2. Allowance budget: each payer grants an independent budget to each charger, so one charger cannot spend another charger's allowance.
  3. Timelocked reductions + governance unregister: a payer can walk away after the delay; governance can disable an explicitly registered service charger via unregisterOperationalFeeCharger.
  4. Guards: the charge path has no external-call surface, and the payer must not be suspended or under active PartyA liquidation.

Fee Source (Perps-Core Payer + Free-First)

In perps-core, the fee source is simply the payer address passed to chargeOperationalFee(payer, amount). Core does not derive the payer from a quote, the signer, the relayed operation, or any account hierarchy.

For that named payer, core checks allowance[payer][charger], debits balances[payer] first, then falls back to allocatedBalances[payer] if free balance is not enough. The charged amount is credited to the charger's configured receiver.

AccountLayer accounts: users operating through SubAccounts or virtual accounts may want operational fees to come from a parent SubAccount instead of the VA that appears in the signed operation. Perps-core will not resolve that relationship. The solver or relayer should determine the intended payer before calling core, whether in service code or an on-chain relayer contract, and pass that address to chargeOperationalFee.

Example: AccountLayer VA charged to parent SubAccount

A user has a SubAccount SA and a virtual account VA-BTC-Long. The signed operation acts on the VA, but the integration wants the fee to come from the parent SubAccount.

  1. The user grants allowance[SA][relayer] = N.
  2. A registered relayer receives a "remove margin from VA-BTC-Long" signed op.
  3. The relayer determines VA-BTC-Long's parent SubAccount before charging and calls chargeOperationalFee(SA, fee). The fee is drawn from SA's free balance; no VA-BTC-Long margin is touched.
  4. The relayer submits InstantLayer.executeBatch for the signed operation.

Solvency

chargeOperationalFee is hierarchy-blind and signature-free: it always debits the named payer free-first, and the allocated remainder (if any) is bounded by a balance-only guard. It never runs a full liquidatability check, because the charge entrypoint carries no Muon upnl signature for the payer.

This relaxed check is intentional for operational fees. Solvers and relayers are trusted charging parties (registered PartyBs or governance-registered services), these fees are expected to be small per-operation amounts, and the payer's allowance caps the total spend. Core therefore checks that any allocated remainder exists, but does not require a full liquidatability signature for this path.

Portion Source Solvency check
Free balance balances[payer] None (not margin)
Allocated remainder allocatedBalances[payer] Balance-only guard (allocatedBalances[payer] >= remainder); no full liquidatability check (no sig available)

This is the same treatment for every charger, including the solver. The solver's separate per-quote rate solverFee, charged inside the fee-aware trading methods, is the only fee that folds into the Muon _requireSolventAfterSolverFee liquidatability check (see Solver Fees).

Timelocked Reductions

Operational-fee allowance is a standing budget from a payer to a charger. Increasing that budget is immediate, so a user can top up a solver or relayer whenever more operational-fee room is needed.

Reducing or revoking the budget is delayed. During that delay, the previous allowance remains active. This prevents a payer from approving an operation, letting a trusted charger take on the work, and then revoking the budget before the small operational fee can be collected.

Once the delay has passed, the reduction is handled through lazy evaluation. View calls report the lower effective allowance automatically, and the next state-changing use of that allowance folds the pending value into storage before continuing. The user does not need a second transaction. If the new allowance is already consumed, future charges simply stop until the payer raises the budget again.

Priority Multiplier

Each payer can attach a fee multiplier to a charger's allowance. The default is 10000, or 1x. A higher value is a priority signal for trusted chargers: if a relayer or solver cannot handle every request, it can prefer work from payers who accepted a higher multiplier.

The multiplier does not replace the allowance. The allowance is still the hard spend budget, and the charger can never draw more than the payer approved.

Gasless Grant

Operational-fee approval is a normal core call. To grant an allowance without gas, the user signs an InstantLayer SignedOperation targeting the approval call.

A relayer can include the allowance or multiplier increase first, then charge its operational fee in the same transaction, so the setup itself can be gasless.

Charging While Relaying

When a relayer pays gas for a user's signed operation, it can collect the operational fee in that same transaction. The transaction charges the selected payer, then executes the signed operation. If execution reverts, the charge reverts with it.
