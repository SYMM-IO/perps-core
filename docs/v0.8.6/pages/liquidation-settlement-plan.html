Exact Liquidation Settlement Plan

A PartyA liquidation now decides what every counterparty pays or receives in one place: the close that brings the position count to zero, from on-chain totals. What the buckets pay out is exactly the liquidated allocation, a solver is never billed more than the amount checked against its allocation, and no wei is created or lost.

Summary

When a PartyA is liquidated, its positions are closed at the signed liquidation prices and the result is collected per PartyB into a settlement bucket: the net PnL PartyA owes or is owed, and the CVA the PartyB gets back. The liquidation type decides what the trader’s collateral can cover. NORMAL means the shortfall is within the locked LF, so PartyBs are made whole and the remaining LF pays the liquidator and the insurance vault. LATE means the LF is gone and part of the CVA is consumed. OVERDUE means the CVA is gone as well and part of the losses stays uncovered.

In LATE and OVERDUE the amount that is missing has to be shared. v0.8.6 shares it in a single plan step from on-chain totals, instead of quote by quote against the signed total loss, and settles the rounding allowance in the same step. The plan is final when the last position closes; settlement only pays it.

How the plan used to be built

Each closed quote adjusted its PartyB’s bucket on its own, as it closed:

  • LATE: the quote’s CVA was reduced by cva_i * deficit / lockedCva.
  • OVERDUE: a losing quote’s loss was reduced by loss_i * deficit / totalUnrealizedLoss, with totalUnrealizedLoss taken from the Muon payload. Winning quotes were counted in full.

When a PartyB’s last position closed, its bucket’s net expected amount was checked against the PartyB’s allocation and counted into PartyA’s accumulated uPNL, which the dispute check compares with the signed value. Settlement, however, paid the bucket’s actual amount: the sum of the reduced quote amounts. The rounding allowance was applied later still, bucket by bucket at settlement time.

Three problems in one path

  1. Gross sharing versus a net check. A solver holding a winning and a losing quote against the trader has a small net claim but a large gross loss. The OVERDUE reduction was applied to the gross loss, so the solver’s planned debit could exceed the net amount the close step had verified against its allocation. An isolated solver short of the planned debit was silently capped at settlement while the other solvers were paid in full, and the difference was created out of nothing without necessarily marking the liquidation disputed. This is a possible accounting path, not a measured incidence rate for live or randomly generated liquidations.
  2. Per-quote floors against an off-chain denominator. Every quote dropped the fraction of its share, so the shares could fail to sum to the deficit, leaving smallest-unit accounting differences. The denominator was a signed field, and a zero there divided by zero. The frequency depends on the inputs and is not established here.
  3. Lazy rounding. The accepted rounding difference was recorded at the last close but consumed at settlement, and the correction for an overstated signed profit was taken from the trader’s deferred balance only, which is zero in the legacy flow. The uncollected part, up to three units per position, was created.

Close step: raw buckets

liquidatePositionsPartyA no longer reduces anything. A PartyB’s bucket opens at its first closed quote and is recorded in partyALiquidationPartyBs, in open order. Every closed quote adds its signed PnL with funding to the bucket’s expected and actual amounts and, in NORMAL and LATE, its raw locked CVA.

When the PartyB has no position left against this PartyA, the bucket is counted. A loss is counted in full. A gain is counted up to the PartyB’s allocation, and the bucket’s actual amount is set to that counted value, so a payer never pays more than what was checked here. For isolated per-PartyA allocation, deallocation, allocation transfer and PartyB liquidation against this liquidated PartyA are blocked. Cross mode instead uses a shared bucket: reserve-aware availability protects the pending claim, but settlement still requires sufficient raw allocation or a prior positive-uPnL top-up.

Plan step: the last close

The close that brings partyAPositionsCount to zero compares the accumulated uPNL with the signed one, as described in Liquidation Rounding and Funding Aggregate Repair. A difference above three units per position disputes the liquidation. An accepted difference settles at the smaller amount supported by both calculations, and that reduction is applied to the buckets now, in open order, with each bucket’s reserve contribution following. The buckets then receive their final amounts according to the liquidation type.

Invariant. After the plan step, the liquidator fee, the vault credit and the sum over buckets of CVA returned minus the amount collected equal exactly the allocation finalization consumes. Nothing is created or stranded.

NORMAL

The liquidator fee and the vault credit were sized at liquidation start from the signed uPNL, while the buckets settle at the capped on-chain amount. The difference between the two is at most the rounding allowance and is settled explicitly:

  • If the capped uPNL is above the signed one, the difference is collateral the buckets do not need. It is moved out of the allocation into PartyA’s deferred balance, which finalization returns to PartyA.
  • If it is below, the difference is taken from deferred balance first, then the actual remaining liquidator fee, then available insurance-vault balance. The liquidation is disputed if those sources cannot cover it. A small configured fee cap can contribute, but is not the only cause of a shortfall.

LATE and OVERDUE

Both types start from the same number: the collateral left once every bucket has been settled at its planned amount, which is PartyA’s allocation plus its net uPNL on chain. 

resources = allocation + cappedUpnl
  • Resources are positive. They are returned as CVA, pro rata to each bucket’s raw locked CVA, with a running remainder: every bucket takes the floor of its share and the last bucket takes what is left. Anything above the total CVA is set aside for PartyA with its deferred balance. This is the LATE case.
  • Resources are negative. The uncovered loss is shared pro rata across the buckets PartyA owes, in proportion to their net claims, with the same running remainder, and no CVA is returned. Payers keep their counted amount. This is the OVERDUE case.

The signed totalUnrealizedLoss is no longer read and the stored deficit is informational: both distributions are exact to the unit because their denominators are on-chain sums.

Worked examples

Allocation 80. PartyB1 holds a winning and a losing quote of 100 each (net 0). PartyB2 holds a losing quote of 200.

net claims            PartyB1 0, PartyB2 200
resources             80 - 200 = -120
PartyB1 pays          0
PartyB2 receives      200 - 120 = 80   (the whole allocation)

Under gross sharing the deficit was 40% of every losing quote: PartyB1 was billed 40 it had never been checked for, PartyB2 was credited 120, and whatever PartyB1 could not pay was created.

Allocation 80. PartyB1 holds a winning quote of 50 (counted at 50). PartyB2 holds a losing quote of 200.

resources             80 + 50 - 200 = -70
PartyB1 pays          50
PartyB2 receives      200 - 70 = 130   (allocation plus PartyB1's payment)

Settlement, disputes and takeover

settlePartyALiquidation pays the stored amounts, in any batches. If an isolated PartyB’s allocation, with its CVA credited back, is below its planned amount, the transaction reverts with LiquidationFacet: Settlement exceeds PartyB allocation instead of paying less. Under the plan this cannot happen, so the check is an invariant rather than a flow; if a dispute resolution writes an amount the allocation cannot cover, the clearing-house takeover remains the recovery. A cross-mode PartyB keeps the existing LiquidationFacet: Settle cross partyB uPNL first requirement.

A disputed liquidation still gets a plan, built on the signed uPNL rather than the on-chain one, so a resolver that overrides some buckets finds the others sized as before. The resolver’s amounts are final; the plan step does not run again. Takeover abandons the buckets and clears the recorded PartyB list with the rest of the liquidation state.

What changes for solvers

  • The OVERDUE deficit is shared on each solver’s net liquidation bucket instead of gross per-quote losses. Ordinary closes have narrower per-close funding/PnL netting, not this cross-position liquidation-bucket netting. A solver that is flat against the trader absorbs nothing; solvers with net claims absorb proportionally more than before, because the flat solvers’ losing legs leave the base.
  • A payer is never debited beyond the amount counted against its allocation when its bucket closed.
  • NORMAL payouts are unchanged. LATE CVA returns are the same policy as before and now exact to the unit.
  • Settlement amounts are final when the last position closes: getSettlementStates returns what will be paid, not a provisional value.

Storage, ABI and integration notes

  1. One mapping, partyALiquidationPartyBs, is appended to AccountStorage.Layout. It is cleared at settlement completion, takeover and the next liquidation start. No existing slot moves and no public signature changes; the LiquidationSettlementState tuple and every event keep their shape.
  2. partyALiquidationRoundingReduction is only consulted for liquidations that were already past their last close when this release was deployed; new liquidations never store a value there.
  3. Muon payloads are unchanged. totalUnrealizedLoss stays signed and stored but is not used by the process.
  4. Indexers that reconstructed OVERDUE amounts from the per-quote formula will disagree with the buckets; read getSettlementStates after the last close, or the settlement events.
  5. A settlement that used to pay an isolated PartyB less than planned now reverts. Liquidation bots should treat Settlement exceeds PartyB allocation as a signal for the dispute role, not retry.
