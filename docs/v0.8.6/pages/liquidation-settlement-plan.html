PartyA Liquidation Settlement and Rounding

v0.8.6 calculates a PartyA liquidation’s payments once, when the last position closes. The contract checks its own per-PartyB total against Muon’s signed uPNL, absorbs the rounding dust between them, and fixes what each PartyB pays or receives. Solvers can read that plan before settlement starts, except in single-step liquidation, which closes, plans and settles in one transaction.

A LATE liquidation in numbers

Take the historical-allocation regression case, shown in whole collateral units: PartyA has 100 allocated at the signed liquidation point, a loss of 90, locked CVA of 20 and locked liquidation fee (LF) of 10. Another 50 is allocated after that signed point. There is one PartyB, no funding and no pending quotes.

Liquidation sets the later 50 aside for PartyA. Of the original 100, PartyB receives 90 for the trading loss and the remaining 10 as CVA. The liquidator receives no fee. PartyA finishes with the deferred 50 restored to its allocation. The extra collateral does not change this LATE liquidation into NORMAL.

This is what the plan makes explicit: which allocation funds settlement, what each PartyB receives, and what returns to PartyA. The same planning code serves the legacy and snapshot liquidation entrypoints.

Read amounts from PartyA’s perspective

A settlement bucket combines all closed positions between this PartyA and one PartyB, including funding. Both expectedAmount and actualAmount use PartyA’s perspective: positive means PartyB pays; negative means PartyB receives. CVA is recorded separately and credited to PartyB before the payment is applied.

Fields returned by getSettlementStates
Field Meaning
actualAmount The current planned net payment. It can change during closing, rounding, loss sharing or a privileged dispute override.
expectedAmount PnL after funding, summed over the bucket’s quotes. Trimming rounding dust changes it by the same amount as actualAmount.
cva The CVA credit to PartyB; raw during closing, reduced by the plan where required.
pending The bucket still awaits settlement. This does not mean all of PartyA’s positions have closed or that settlement is allowed.

PartyB’s net allocation change at settlement is cva - actualAmount. For the LATE case above, actualAmount = -90 and cva = 10, so PartyB gains 100.

Collect one bucket per PartyB

Each closed quote contributes its PnL minus accumulated funding to its PartyB’s bucket. NORMAL and LATE buckets also accumulate the quote’s locked CVA; OVERDUE buckets do not. A bucket enters partyALiquidationPartyBs when its first quote closes. This first-close order is the order used later for rounding and distributions.

Once a PartyB has no open position left against PartyA, its net amount enters partyAAccumulatedUpnl. Negative amounts are counted in full. Positive amounts are capped at that PartyB’s allocation, before CVA is credited back, and actualAmount is set to the capped amount. Automatic planning can reduce that payment further; it does not increase it.

Isolated mode uses the allocation for this PartyA. Cross mode uses the shared allocation at address(0); its settlement reserve follows positive planned payments. The reserve reduces available balance but does not itself supply collateral. See Cross PartyB Liquidation Reserve for the protection and top-up path.

Fix the plan at the last close

The close that brings partyAPositionsCount to zero also fixes every bucket’s final amount, in three steps within the same transaction:

  1. Check. Compare the counted uPNL with the signed uPNL. They can differ by a few raw units of rounding dust; a difference above three raw units per starting position marks the liquidation disputed. Why they differ
  2. Absorb the dust. Within that allowance, settle at the total closer to zero and trim those few raw units from the buckets. How the dust is settled
  3. Reconcile or distribute. NORMAL reconciles the liquidator fee and vault credit with the settled total. LATE and OVERDUE share out what PartyA’s allocation can cover.
Settlement order never changes a payout. Every amount is stored at the last close, so settling PartyBs in one batch or several, in any order, pays the same amounts. Only a privileged dispute override changes them. A liquidation that passed its last close on version_0.8.6.4 through version_0.8.6.7 is the exception; see Storage and in-flight liquidations. The order positions close in matters only at the dust level: it decides which PartyB gives up a rounding unit, or gets the leftover unit of a share that does not divide evenly. Splitting 2 raw units of CVA across three PartyBs with equal CVA gives 0, 1 and 1, with the rounded-down share going to the PartyB that closed first.

Rounding dust: why Muon and the contract differ by a few units

This is dust, not a payout rule. Balances are stored with 18 decimals, so a raw unit is 10−18 of a collateral token, and the allowance is three raw units per position. Even a liquidation with 1,000 positions allows at most 3,000 raw units, while the smallest amount of a 6-decimal collateral such as USDC is 1012 raw units. This section and the next exist so that dust settles cleanly: no funds are created or destroyed, and a rounding-sized difference does not stop the liquidation. No payout changes by an amount anyone would notice.

At the last close the contract holds two figures for PartyA’s uPNL. The signed uPNL is what Muon reported when liquidation started. The counted uPNL is the sum of the buckets after every position closed, with each payer counted at no more than its allocation. Both use the same prices and positions, but they round at different points.

Muon calculates PnL per group of positions. The contract calculates it per quote and adds the results. Integer division drops the fraction toward zero, so cut(4.8) = 4 and cut(−4.8) = −4, and dropping fractions before adding gives a different total from adding first: 

Quote by quote: cut(4.8) + cut(5.8) = 4 + 5 = 9
Group total:    cut(4.8 + 5.8)      = cut(10.6) = 10

Price PnL rounding loses less than one raw unit per quote and funding rounding less than two, so the contract allows three raw units per position. The position count is captured when liquidation starts, readable with liquidationStartPositionCount, which keeps the allowance fixed when positions close in several batches. A difference equal to the allowance is accepted; a larger one is not rounding, and it disputes the liquidation.

The bound holds only when the stored funding totals are exact and Muon prices from exact notional. The contract checks the number; it cannot check those inputs. See Keep funding totals exact and Give Muon exact notional.

Rounding dust: settle at the total closer to zero

Everything in this section moves only the few raw units described above. Within the allowance, the liquidation settles at whichever of the two totals is closer to zero. If one total is a profit for PartyA and the other a loss, it settles at zero. This page calls the settled total the capped uPNL. After a plan step that did not dispute the liquidation, getLiquidatedStateOfPartyA returns it as partyAAccumulatedUpnl.

That is the amount both calculations support. If Muon says PartyA lost 100 and the buckets add up to a loss of 102, both agree PartyA lost at least 100, so 100 is paid out. If the totals disagree about who owes whom, the only amount both support is zero.

PartyA’s uPNL in raw units, for a liquidation that started with three positions (allowance 9)
Signed (Muon) Counted (buckets) Settles at What happens to the buckets
−100 −102 −100 2 units of loss come off the buckets
−103 −102 −102 Nothing: they already add up to −102
+1 −2 0 2 units of loss come off the buckets
−92 −102 None The difference of 10 is above the allowance, so the liquidation is disputed

When the counted total is the one further from zero, the buckets must shrink to match. The contract walks them in first-close order and takes the gap only from buckets on the same side as the counted total: a loss gap comes off buckets where PartyA owes the PartyB, and a profit gap comes off buckets where the PartyB pays. Each bucket gives up as much as it can, down to zero but never past it, and the next bucket covers the rest. Buckets on the other side keep their amounts.

PartyB2 gives up its single raw unit because it is the first loss-side bucket in close order, not because of its size. If PartyB3 had closed before PartyB2, it would have absorbed both units and PartyB2 would keep its 1. The gap is at most three raw units per position, so this order rule only decides who absorbs rounding dust. Real losses are shared in proportion to net claims; see LATE and OVERDUE.

Each trimmed bucket’s expectedAmount changes by the same amount, so settlement does not report the rounding unit as a funding haircut. A cross PartyB’s settlement reserve is re-synced to the trimmed payment. When the signed total is the one further from zero, as in the second case, the buckets already add up to the settled total and stay as they are; NORMAL then reconciles the fee and vault credit, as described next.

NORMAL: full CVA, with the dust reconciled

At start, NORMAL means the available-balance shortfall is strictly less than the locked LF. The remaining LF funds the liquidator fee, capped by the configured per-position limit; excess is credited to the insurance vault. Buckets retain their full locked CVA.

The fee and vault credit were sized from the signed uPNL. When rounding dust moves the settled total, the plan squares them up by the same few raw units:

  • Capped uPNL above the signed uPNL: move the difference from PartyA’s allocation to its deferred balance, which returns to its allocation at finalization. In the second case above, the buckets pay a loss of 102 while the fee was sized for a loss of 103, so 1 raw unit moves to PartyA’s deferred balance.
  • Capped uPNL below the signed uPNL: cover the difference from deferred balance first, then the remaining liquidator fee, then the insurance vault’s available balance.
  • Those sources are insufficient: mark the liquidation disputed and block ordinary settlement. The plan does not silently create the missing collateral, and it keeps whatever it already took from the deferred balance, the liquidator fee and the vault.

The vault recovery can use its existing balance, not only the credit from this liquidation. NORMAL therefore keeps the full-CVA policy; rounding moves PartyB payments, PartyA’s returned balance, the liquidator fee and the vault balance only by that dust.

LATE and OVERDUE: distribute what remains

The type is fixed at liquidation start. A shortfall from locked LF through locked LF plus CVA, inclusive, is LATE; a larger shortfall is OVERDUE. At the exact LF boundary, LATE consumes no CVA. At the LF-plus-CVA boundary, it consumes all CVA. Those boundaries describe the signed starting state; rounding can change the final CVA returned. Neither type pays a liquidator fee through this path.

Both types use the same distribution routine. For a non-disputed plan, its starting pool is resources = allocation + cappedUpnl. Here allocation excludes collateral already set aside at start. The sign of resources selects the distribution; it does not reclassify the stored liquidation type.

  • Resources above zero: return up to the total bucket CVA, weighted by raw bucket CVA. Set any excess aside for PartyA. OVERDUE has no raw bucket CVA, so a positive remainder goes to PartyA.
  • Resources equal zero: return no CVA and apply no additional loss reduction.
  • Resources below zero: return no CVA. Reduce negative actualAmount magnitudes in proportion to the net claims, up to the total of those claims. Positive buckets keep their counted payment.

LATE never reaches the last case. Its shortfall is at most locked LF plus CVA, so its resources stay at zero or above and it never reduces PnL claims; only OVERDUE can.

Each share uses the remaining pool or deficit divided by the remaining eligible weight. After each bucket, both are reduced. The last eligible bucket takes the remainder, even if other ineligible buckets follow it. This makes the shares add up exactly, although first-close order can affect individual shares by integer rounding.

The distribution does not use the stored deficit or signed totalUnrealizedLoss as its denominator. The latter remains part of signature verification and stored liquidation details; it must still be supplied in the Muon payload.

Why net claims change the payout

Consider a simplified mixed-book case with 80 allocated, no funding and signed uPNL equal to the counted total. Against PartyB1, PartyA wins 100 on one quote and loses 100 on another. Against PartyB2, PartyA loses 200. The net buckets are 0 and −200, so resources are 80 - 200 = -120.

Only PartyB2 has a net claim. It absorbs the 120 uncovered loss and receives 80; PartyB1 pays nothing. Under the v0.8.5 gross-loss formula, the 120 deficit was spread over 300 of losing quotes. That reduced PartyB1’s losing leg by 40, turning its flat book into a 40 payment, while PartyB2 received 120. If PartyB1 could not fund the 40, the isolated settlement cap left the credits unfunded.

If PartyB1 instead owes a counted 50 and PartyB2 still has a 200 claim, resources are 80 + 50 - 200 = -70. PartyB1 pays 50 and PartyB2 receives 130: the allocation plus the collected payment. A payer’s payment is not increased to absorb someone else’s haircut.

Settlement and dispute recovery

settlePartyALiquidation requires an active liquidation, no takeover, no remaining open or pending positions, and a cleared dispute flag. Each listed PartyB must have a pending bucket and must not be undergoing cross liquidation. The external entrypoint also checks the liquidation pause.

Settlement credits CVA first, then applies the stored payment. If a positive payment exceeds the resulting allocation, the whole transaction reverts:

  • LiquidationFacet: Settlement exceeds PartyB allocation for an isolated PartyB. Check the plan, a dispute override, or an allocation that changed after the bucket was counted; the call does not mark a dispute automatically, and retrying unchanged state cannot fix it.
  • LiquidationFacet: Settle cross partyB uPNL first for a cross PartyB. Where eligible, settlePartyBUpnlForLiquidation converts positive uPNL into allocation before retrying.

While PartyA is liquidated, an isolated PartyB cannot deallocate from it, transfer allocation from or to it, or be liquidated against it. Two paths can still change the allocation a counted bucket settles against. softPartyBLiquidation, called with SOFT_LIQUIDATOR_ROLE, debits it without checking for an active PartyA liquidation. activateCrossPartyB switches an isolated PartyB to its shared cross allocation without checking for pending settlement; the allocation it held for this PartyA stays behind, and the bucket then settles against the shared one. A cross PartyB’s shared allocation can also fall through ordinary cross activity.

A revert fails only its own batch, so settle the other PartyBs in separate calls. The liquidation cannot finish until the blocked bucket settles, for example after an isolated PartyB tops up its allocation with allocateForPartyB or after a dispute override, or the Clearing House takes over.

If the uPNL mismatch exceeds the allowance, the liquidation is left disputed. LATE and OVERDUE still build the distribution, using signed uPNL for resources. NORMAL changes nothing: the buckets keep their counted amounts, with no trim and no fee or vault reconciliation. Either way this is a blocked plan for review, not a guarantee that the resulting buckets conserve value or can be paid.

A caller with DISPUTE_ROLE can replace selected pending buckets’ actual amounts and set the dispute flag. Unlisted buckets, CVA and expected amounts remain as stored; the override does not rerun distribution or check a global accounting identity. After the last close, settlement uses the overridden amounts. The resolver also accepts pending buckets before that close, when later closing and planning can still change them.

Clearing House takeover replaces ordinary settlement. Takeover settlement clears the remaining buckets and the recorded PartyB list. See Liquidation Dispute Resolution Guards and Explicit Clearing House Settlements for the related paths.

Read the plan at the right time

  • Before the last close, getSettlementStates returns amounts still being built. After it, check the disputed flag from getLiquidatedStateOfPartyA before treating them as payable. A dispute override can change them again.
  • Keep the PartyB list from quotes or events. Closing can remove empty connections, so getConnectedPartyBs is not a reliable list of unsettled buckets. The recorded list, partyALiquidationPartyBs, has no public getter; each bucket’s PartyB appears in QuoteLiquidationFundingCalculated events.
  • After a bucket settles, its stored state is deleted. Use SettlePartyALiquidation events for completed payments; the extended event includes allocation keys and CVA amounts.
  • Indexers must use the stored net plan rather than reconstruct OVERDUE payouts with the v0.8.5 gross per-quote formula. Ordinary close settlement uses a separate netting path.

What the accounting identity includes

For the automatic, non-disputed plan, account for all destinations together. The liquidated allocation equals the sum of PartyB allocation changes, the final liquidator fee, the net insurance-vault credit, and the change in PartyA’s deferred balance during planning. To measure that change, read getPartyADeferredBalance just before and just after the last close. Single-step liquidation finalizes in the same transaction, so there the change cannot be read this way.

A deferred increase is a return to PartyA; a deferred decrease contributes existing collateral to the payout. Net vault credit means the start credit minus any rounding recovery. Omitting either change can make a balanced plan appear to create or lose funds.

Pending-fee reimbursements are separate from that allocation identity. At finalization they return to PartyA’s allocation for NORMAL, or move to liquidation escrow for LATE and OVERDUE. Deferred funds return to PartyA’s allocation in all three types. A privileged override needs its own accounting review.

Keep funding totals exact

Each PartyA/PartyB/symbol/side group stores the sum of its open quotes’ weighted paid funding, each contribution truncated toward zero. Each PartyB also keeps a global total per symbol and side. These totals feed the funding debt in the uPNL views Muon reads, so drift in them becomes drift in the signed uPNL.

A partial close removes the quote’s old contribution minus its new one, so the stored total always equals what the remaining quotes contribute: 

Removed on a partial close = cut(old open amount × funding) − cut(new open amount × funding)

When a group has no open amount left, its funding total is set to zero, so a reopened group starts clean. Removing cut(closed amount × funding) for each slice instead can leave dust: a 60% close followed by a 40% close can leave one raw unit after the quote has fully closed.

Totals that drifted before this rule need a one-time repair. scripts/resyncAggregateFunding.ts recalculates the groups listed in a GROUPS_FILE JSON array, at one paused block and with the contract’s arithmetic, against the diamond given as SYMMIO. It does not find drifted groups itself. An operator with MIGRATION_ROLE then submits the results to resyncAggregateFunding on MigrationFacet, which:

  • requires a global pause;
  • reverts the whole batch if any group’s PartyA is in liquidation or its PartyB is liquidating;
  • reverts the whole batch if a group’s stored totals no longer match the expected old values, so a stale calculation cannot land;
  • treats a repair that was already applied as a no-op;
  • changes funding totals, not positions or balances, and increments both parties’ uPNL counters, so Muon signatures over the old totals stop verifying.

Give Muon exact notional

The original uPNL views return an average open price. Calculating that average divides notional by amount and drops the remainder, and multiplying the average back by the amount cannot restore it: 

Average-price LONG PnL: (price − truncated average) × amount / 1e18
Exact LONG PnL:         (price × amount − exact notional) / 1e18

Three views return the stored aggregatedNotional instead: getPartyAExactNotionalUpnlData, getPartyBExactNotionalUpnlData and getPartyBGlobalExactNotionalUpnlData. The average-price views remain unchanged. Muon uses the exact path only for contracts listed in its MUON_EXACT_NOTIONAL_CONTRACTS allowlist, keyed by chain ID. List a deployment only once it exposes these views; v0.8.5 contracts do not.

Storage and in-flight liquidations

Three mappings are appended to the end of AccountStorage.Layout: liquidationStartPositionCounts, partyALiquidationRoundingReduction and partyALiquidationPartyBs. Each liquidation start records the position count and clears the other two. Completion clears all three; takeover settlement clears the count and the PartyB list. A takeover start and a dispute override also clear the rounding reduction.

Finish every active PartyA liquidation, including Clearing House takeovers, before switching implementations. The new code does not handle a liquidation that started on older code and closes after the upgrade. Its recorded PartyB list misses every bucket that opened before the upgrade, those buckets can carry the older code’s per-quote CVA and loss cuts, and the plan step still counts them in resources. Depending on the type, the book and the order positions close in:

  • PartyBs can be paid more than PartyA’s allocation, with the difference taken from other users’ collateral.
  • PartyBs can be paid less, leaving collateral in the diamond that no account owns.
  • The last close can revert, with LiquidationFacet: Invalid rounding cap or, when PartyA is net in profit, with an arithmetic underflow; and an isolated payer short on allocation can revert at settlement.

A wrong plan settles without reverting, and anyone can call settlement, so act before the last close. Start a Clearing House takeover, or have the DISPUTE_ROLE holder set the dispute flag with resolveLiquidationDispute; the plan step never clears that flag, so settlement stays blocked. A takeover is also the way out of a liquidation that cannot finish.

A liquidation with no positions closed before the upgrade records its buckets normally. One started on v0.8.5, or on a v0.8.6 build before version_0.8.6.2, also has no start position count, so its allowance is zero and any rounding gap disputes it. The new code backfills neither the list nor the count. A v0.8.5 bucket owed by a cross PartyB also stays in that PartyB’s settlement reserve after settlement or takeover: v0.8.5 kept no per-PartyA record of it, so nothing releases it, and it keeps reducing the PartyB’s available balance.

A liquidation that passed its last close on older code keeps its old per-quote amounts, and an isolated PartyB whose allocation is below its planned amount now reverts at settlement instead of being capped. Builds version_0.8.6.4 through version_0.8.6.7 also stored a rounding reduction for settlement to apply, and settlement still applies it bucket by bucket in settlement order, which is why settlement order can change these payouts. Each pending bucket on the reduction’s side absorbs what it can from its actualAmount, which an older OVERDUE cut may have moved to zero or across it, and passes the rest on. The final settlement reverts with LiquidationFacet: Invalid rounding cap only if none of them can absorb it. A dispute override or a takeover clears the reduction.

In LATE and OVERDUE, the last close can walk every recorded PartyB three times in one transaction, even when positions were closed in batches: once to trim rounding dust and twice to distribute. NORMAL walks them at most once, and only while trimming a rounding gap. Validate the LATE and OVERDUE worst case on the target deployment; this page supplies no gas bound.

Upgrade and repair order

The rounding allowance is safe only once funding totals are repaired and Muon prices from exact notional. Rehearse first and finish in-flight liquidations while the system is live; do the rest in one paused window:

  1. Rehearse on a recent fork of the target deployment. Record the chain, diamond, facet and library addresses. Build the facet list from compiled bytecode and artifact linkReferences: when a library gets a new address, every facet that links it must be redeployed. Check each selector with facetAddress(bytes4) before building the cut. Add absent selectors and Replace existing ones. Remove only selectors you mean to retire, because removing an absent selector reverts the whole cut.
  2. Finish active PartyA and PartyB liquidations, including takeovers. The repair rejects liquidating parties, and a global pause blocks liquidation settlement. Resolve any that cannot finish through dispute resolution or the Clearing House.
  3. Pause globally and confirm that no PartyA or PartyB liquidation or takeover is still active. If one is, unpause, finish it as in step 2 and pause again: a PartyA liquidation is the in-flight case, and the repair reverts for any group whose parties are liquidating. Then apply the cut, and confirm that resyncAggregateFunding and the three exact-notional views respond through the diamond.
  4. List the groups to repair in GROUPS_FILE, including emptied groups that still hold leftover funding, which open positions alone will not reveal, and set SYMMIO to the diamond. Run scripts/resyncAggregateFunding.ts read-only, which is its default, at the paused block. Review the proposed totals.
  5. Submit the repairs in batches, either as the printed governance calldata or with EXECUTE=true and CONFIRM_CHAIN_ID. Verify the stored totals afterwards.
  6. Add the chain ID and diamond address to Muon’s MUON_EXACT_NOTIONAL_CONTRACTS and restart Muon.
  7. Check Muon’s exact-notional results against the repaired totals, then unpause.
